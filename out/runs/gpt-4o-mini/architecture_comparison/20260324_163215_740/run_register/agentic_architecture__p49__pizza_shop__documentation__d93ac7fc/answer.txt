The **pairwise function** in the context of Sensei-check refers to a mechanism used in metamorphic testing to compare pairs of conversations. Here's a breakdown of how it works:

### Overview of Pairwise Functionality

1. **Purpose**: 
   - The pairwise function allows you to assert relationships between outputs of two conversations that meet specific conditions. This helps in validating that the chatbot behaves consistently under varying inputs.

2. **Metamorphic Rules**:
   - In Sensei-check, metamorphic rules can target multiple conversations. They compare outputs from pairs of conversations to check how changes in inputs affect the outputs.
   - This approach alleviates the oracle problem by focusing on the relationships between outputs rather than requiring absolute correctness.

### Key Components

- **Conversations**: 
  - You specify how many conversations the rule needs (usually at least two for pairwise comparisons).
  
- **Filters**: 
  - You can use a `when` clause to filter which conversations are eligible for comparison. This ensures that the comparisons are meaningful (e.g., comparing conversations with the same pizza size).

- **Relations**: 
  - The rule can define conditions using `if` and `then` statements to express the expected relationship between the outputs of the two conversations.
  
### Example Syntax

Here’s a simple example of a pairwise rule:

```yaml
name: toppings_monotonicity
description: More toppings increase the total price
conversations: 2
when: conv[0].size == conv[1].size and conv[0].drink == conv[1].drink
if:   len(conv[0].toppings) > len(conv[1].toppings)
then: extract_float(conv[0].price) > extract_float(conv[1].price)
```

### Explanation of the Example

- **Name and Description**: The rule checks if adding more toppings to a pizza increases its price.
- **Conversations**: It requires two conversations for comparison.
- **When Clause**: It filters conversations to ensure they have the same size and drink.
- **If Condition**: It checks if the first conversation has more toppings than the second.
- **Then Condition**: It asserts that the price of the first conversation should be greater than that of the second.

### Conclusion

The pairwise function in Sensei-check is a powerful way to validate chatbot behavior by comparing outputs across multiple conversations. It emphasizes the relationships between outputs, making it easier to identify inconsistencies or errors in the chatbot's responses.
