What I *can* do is help you build the pieces that such an automation would need to produce — namely **valid Sensei profile YAMLs** and the **validation criteria** they must satisfy. That way, whatever script you write just needs to emit YAML matching the contract below.

Here's a quick rundown of what a "valid profile" must respect, so your automation can target it:

- **Top-level sections**: `test_name`, `llm`, `user`, `chatbot`, `conversation`.
- **`user.goals`**: every `{{variable}}` used in a goal string must be declared as a mapping in the same `goals` list, with matching (case-sensitive) name, `type`, `data`, and `function`.
- **`function`** must be one of: `default()`, `random()`, `random(n)`, `random(rand)`, `another()`, `another(n)`, `forward()`, `pairwise()`. `pairwise()` needs ≥2 pairwise variables; `combinations` in `conversation.number` needs ≥2 nested `forward` or ≥2 `pairwise`.
- **`type`** in `user.goals`: only `int`, `float`, `string`, or a custom type defined in `types/`. (No `list`, `time`, `date` in goals.)
- **`chatbot.output`**: primitives + `time(...)`, `date(...)`, `list[...]`, `currency`, `money`, or custom types. Field names here become `conv[i].<field>` in rules.
- **`interaction_style`**: only reserved keywords (`long phrases`, `change your mind`, `change language: [...]`, `make spelling mistakes`, `single questions`, `all questions`, `default`, `random: [...]`).
- **`conversation.number`**: int, `combinations`, `combinations(p)`, or `combinations(p, function)`.
