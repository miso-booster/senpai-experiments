## Quick heads-up on the pairwise requirement ⚠️

You asked for **2 variables with `pairwise()`** so the conversation number can come from the pairwise matrix. Just a small but important detail from the docs: **`pairwise()` needs at least 2 assigned variables to work**, and to define `conversation.number` from a combination matrix you need **a minimum of two pairwise functions declared**. So your setup is spot-on — 2 pairwise variables will do it. 👍

## The profile

Here's a complete, runnable profile. I've made the user rude and rushed, with 4 goals and 4 variables (2 using `pairwise()`):

```yaml
test_name: "rude_impatient_pizza_order"

llm:
  temperature: 0.8
  model: gpt-4o-mini
  model_prov: openai
  format:
    type: text

user:
  language: English
  role: >
    You are a rude, impatient customer ordering pizza over the phone.
    You are in a hurry and get annoyed easily. You speak bluntly, cut to the
    chase, and expect fast answers. You do not say please or thank you, and
    you push the bot to hurry up.
  context:
    - "You are ordering on your way home and have very little time."
    - "You have no patience for small talk or long explanations."
    - "If the bot is slow or asks too many questions, you get irritated."
  goals:
    - "Demand a {{pizza_type}} pizza, {{pizza_size}} size, and tell them to be quick about it."
    - "Order {{number_of_drinks}} cans of {{drink_type}} — and make it fast."
    - "Ask how long the order will take, and complain if it's too long."
    - "Ask for the total price and the order ID."
    - pizza_type:
        function: pairwise()
        type: string
        data: [Margarita, Pepperoni, Hawaiian, Vegetarian, Four cheese]
    - pizza_size:
        function: pairwise()
        type: string
        data: [small, medium, large]
    - number_of_drinks:
        function: forward()
        type: int
        data:
          min: 1
          max: 4
          step: 1
    - drink_type:
        function: forward()
        type: string
        data: [Coke, Sprite, Fanta, Water]

chatbot:
  is_starter: false
  fallback: "I'm sorry, I did not get what you said. Can you rephrase?"
  output:
    - order_summary:
        type: string
        description: "Summary of the pizza and drink order (type, size, quantity, drink)."
    - total_price:
        type: money
        description: "Final price of the order, including currency symbol."
    - estimated_time:
        type: time
        description: "Estimated time until the order is ready."
    - order_id:
        type: string
        description: "The order identifier / order number."

conversation:
  number: combinations(1, pairwise)   # derived from the pairwise matrix (2 pairwise vars)
  goal_style:
    steps: 6
    max_cost: 0.15
  interaction_style:
    - long phrases
    - random:
        - make spelling mistakes
        - single questions
```

## Why this works ✅

- **4 goals** → the four `- "..."` lines at the top of `goals`.
- **4 variables** → `pizza_type`, `pizza_size`, `number_of_drinks`, `drink_type`.
- **2 pairwise variables** → `pizza_type` and `pizza_size` both use `pairwise()`, which satisfies the minimum of two pairwise functions needed to compute `conversation.number` from the matrix. 🎯
- **`combinations(1, pairwise)`** → uses the full pairwise matrix (you can swap `1` for a fraction like `0.6` to sample 60%).
- **Rude & fast persona** → baked into `role` and `context`, plus `long phrases` and `single questions` interaction styles keep it terse and demanding.

## A couple of friendly tips 💡

- If you'd rather have the **full grid** instead of pairwise coverage, swap `pairwise()` for nested `forward()` — but then you'd lose the pairwise-matrix conversation number.
- The `money` and `time` types in `chatbot.output` are great for later **Sensei-check rules** (e.g., `conv[0].total_price > 0`). Just make sure those types exist in your `types/` folder if they're custom. 👀
