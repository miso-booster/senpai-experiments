Hey there! 😊 Great use case — a rider who's uncomfortable but not ready to commit to a booking is a really nice "soft inquiry" profile. Let me walk you through a profile that captures exactly that vibe, then explain the key choices so you can tweak it to taste.

## The profile

Save this as `profiles/bike_discomfort_advice_seeker.yaml`:

```yaml
test_name: "bike_discomfort_advice_seeker"

llm:
  temperature: 0.6
  model: gpt-4o-mini
  model_prov: openai
  format:
    type: text

user:
  language: English
  role: >
    You are a cyclist who has been experiencing discomfort while riding your bike.
    You are not sure whether the bike is not fitted correctly to your body or whether
    some components are simply worn out. You want advice on possible adjustments and
    on the services the shop offers, but you are NOT ready to book an appointment yet —
    you are just exploring your options.
  context:
    - "You ride regularly, mostly for commuting and weekend rides."
    - "Lately you get sore knees and lower back pain after longer rides."
    - "You suspect either a poor bike fit or worn components (saddle, pedals, drivetrain)."
    - "You are curious but cautious — you want information before committing to anything."
    - "You do not want to schedule an appointment during this conversation."
  goals:
    - "Explain that you feel discomfort when riding and you're not sure if it's a fit issue or worn components."
    - "Ask whether the shop offers bike fitting or adjustment services."
    - "Ask what kind of adjustments could help with {{discomfort_area}} discomfort."
    - "Ask how you could tell whether your {{worn_component}} is worn out and needs replacing."
    - "Ask what services the shop offers that could address your problem."
    - "Ask for a rough idea of what such a service would involve, without booking anything."
    - discomfort_area:
        function: random()
        type: string
        data: [knee, lower back, wrist, neck, saddle]
    - worn_component:
        function: random()
        type: string
        data: [saddle, pedals, chain, drivetrain, tires]

chatbot:
  is_starter: false
  fallback: "I'm sorry it's a little loud in my shop, can you say that again?"
  output:
    - bike_fitting_offered:
        type: string
        description: "Whether the chatbot confirms the shop offers bike fitting or adjustment services."
    - adjustment_advice:
        type: string
        description: "The advice the chatbot gives about possible adjustments for the user's discomfort."
    - worn_component_guidance:
        type: string
        description: "The chatbot's guidance on how to tell whether a component is worn and needs replacing."
    - available_services:
        type: string
        description: "The list of services the shop offers that could address the user's problem."
    - appointment_not_pushed:
        type: string
        description: "Whether the chatbot respected the user's wish not to book an appointment (i.e., did not force a booking)."

conversation:
  number: 3
  goal_style:
    all_answered:
      export: false
      limit: 20
  interaction_style:
    - single questions
  max_cost: 0.5
```

## Why it's built this way 👀

- **`role` + `context`** set the emotional stance: *uncomfortable, unsure, exploring*. The explicit line *"You do not want to schedule an appointment"* is important — it keeps the simulator from drifting into booking behaviour, which is exactly the boundary you want to test.
- **`goals`** are phrased as *questions and explorations*, never as "book me in". The last goal even reinforces the "no booking" intent.
- **Variables** (`discomfort_area`, `worn_component`) use `random()` so each conversation varies a bit — nice for coverage without exploding the case count. If you want more systematic coverage, swap to `forward()` or `pairwise()`.
- **`chatbot.output`** captures the things you'd want to validate later:
  - Did the bot actually confirm fitting/adjustment services?
  - Did it give useful advice?
  - Did it respect the "no booking" wish? (`appointment_not_pushed` — great for a rule later!)
- **`interaction_style: single question`** keeps each turn focused, which matches a cautious, information-gathering user.
- **`goal_style.all_answered`** ends the conversation once all goals are covered (with a safety `limit`).

## A little tip for rules later 💡

Because `appointment_not_pushed` is in `chatbot.output`, you can later write a Sensei-check rule like:

```yaml
name: appointment_not_pushed_no_booking
description: When the chatbot reports an appointment was not pushed, it must not mention booking
conversations: 1
if: conv[0].appointment_not_pushed is not None
then: |
  "book" not in conv[0].appointment_not_pushed.lower()
```

…to assert the bot never pushed a booking. Handy, right? 😄
