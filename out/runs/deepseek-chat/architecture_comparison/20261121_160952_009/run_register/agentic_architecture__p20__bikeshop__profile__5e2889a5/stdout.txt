Here's a profile for testing a bike shop's appointment handling around wheel changes 🚲. It's built around the `sensei-chat-guide.md` conventions (variables, `forward`/`pairwise`/`another` functions, `chatbot.output` schema, and conversation controls).

```yaml
test_name: "bike_shop_wheel_change_appointment"

llm:
  temperature: 0.7
  model: gpt-4o-mini
  model_prov: openai
  format:
    type: text

user:
  language: English
  role: >
    You are a customer of a bike shop who wants to book an appointment to have
    the wheels on your bike changed. You care about finding a day and time that
    works for you, so you ask about availability and confirm the details.
  context:
    - "You commute by bike daily, so you want the wheel change done soon."
    - "You are flexible but prefer mornings or late afternoons."
    - "You want the shop to confirm the exact date and time of the appointment."
  goals:
    - "Ask to book an appointment to change the wheels of my bike."
    - "Request an appointment on {{appointment_date}} at {{appointment_time}}."
    - "If that slot is not available, ask for the closest alternative on {{alternative_date}}."
    - "Confirm the final appointment date and time with the shop."
    - appointment_date:
        function: forward()
        type: string
        data:
          - tomorrow
          - next Monday
          - next Saturday
          - the 15th of this month
    - appointment_time:
        function: pairwise()
        type: string
        data:
          - "09:00"
          - "11:30"
          - "14:00"
          - "17:30"
    - alternative_date:
        function: pairwise()
        type: string
        data:
          - the day after tomorrow
          - next Wednesday
          - next Friday
          - the 20th of this month

chatbot:
  is_starter: false
  fallback: "I'm sorry, it's a little loud in my shop, can you say that again?"
  output:
    - confirmed_date:
        type: date
        description: "The date of the wheel-change appointment confirmed by the chatbot."
    - confirmed_time:
        type: time("hh:mm")
        description: "The time of the wheel-change appointment confirmed by the chatbot."
    - service_requested:
        type: string
        description: "The service the user asked for, e.g. wheel change."
    - appointment_confirmed:
        type: string
        description: "The chatbot's confirmation message for the appointment, including date and time."
    - alternative_offered:
        type: string
        description: "Any alternative date or time the chatbot offered if the requested slot was unavailable."

conversation:
  number: combinations(1, pairwise)
  max_cost: 0.5
  goal_style:
    all_answered:
      export: true
      limit: 20
  interaction_style:
    - change your mind
    - random:
        - make spelling mistakes
        - single questions
        - long phrases
```

### What this profile does 🎯

- **Simulated user**: a bike-shop customer who wants to **change their wheels** and book an appointment.
- **Date/time variables** (the core of your test):
  - `appointment_date` → `forward()` iterates deterministically through 4 different days.
  - `appointment_time` → `pairwise()` covers all time slots efficiently.
  - `alternative_date` → also `pairwise()`, so the fallback days are exercised alongside the time slots in the pairwise matrix.
- **`conversation.number: combinations(1, pairwise)`** → runs the full pairwise matrix across the two `pairwise()` variables, so you systematically test different day/time combinations.
- **`chatbot.output`** extracts `confirmed_date`, `confirmed_time`, `service_requested`, `appointment_confirmed`, and `alternative_offered` — these are the fields you'll later reference in rules as `conv[i].<field>`.
- **`interaction_style`**: `change your mind` is especially useful here (the docs note it's great for appointment dates), plus a random mix of typos/single questions/long phrases.

### A couple of notes 💡

- I used `time("hh:mm")` and `date` types for the confirmed fields so they're cleanly parseable — handy if you later write a rule like "the confirmed time matches one of the offered slots."
- I set `is_starter: false` (user speaks first). If your bike-shop bot greets first, flip it to `true`.
