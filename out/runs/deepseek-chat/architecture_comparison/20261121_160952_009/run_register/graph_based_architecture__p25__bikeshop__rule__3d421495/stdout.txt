## ⚠️ Quick heads-up before we write the rule

Your `opening_and_closing` field is a **string** describing the shop's hours (e.g., `"10am to 8pm"`). "10 to 20" could mean two things:

1. **Opening hour = 10, closing hour = 20** (i.e., 10:00–20:00) — most likely what you mean 🕙
2. A numeric range check on some value

Since it's a free-text string, the cleanest way to check it is with the **`semantic_content`** helper (LLM-backed), which verifies that the string *means* what you expect. That's much more robust than trying to `extract_float` a phrase like `"10am to 8pm"`.

Also note: `seat_price` is typed as `str` but you want to compare it to `20` — I'll use `extract_float` to pull the number out. Same for `tire` and `tune_up` (even though they're `money`, `extract_float` is the safest way to compare).

---

## ✅ The rule

```yaml
name: bike_shop_prices_and_hours
description: >
  Checks the bike shop profile outputs: opening/closing hours are 10–20,
  tune-up costs 50, seat costs 20, and tyre costs 20.
conversations: 1
oracle: >
  semantic_content(conv[0].opening_and_closing, 'opens at 10 and closes at 20 (10am to 8pm)')
  and extract_float(conv[0].tune_up) == 50
  and extract_float(conv[0].seat_price) == 20
  and extract_float(conv[0].tire) == 20
on-error: >
  Expected hours 10–20, tune_up=50, seat=20, tire=20 — got
  opening_and_closing={conv[0].opening_and_closing},
  tune_up={conv[0].tune_up}, seat_price={conv[0].seat_price}, tire={conv[0].tire}
```

---

## 🔍 Why this works

| Check | Helper | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `opening_and_closing` = 10–20 | `semantic_content` | It's a **string** like `"10am to 8pm"` — semantic check handles phrasing variations ("10:00–20:00", "10 to 8pm", etc.) |
| `tune_up` = 50 | `extract_float` | Pulls the number out of `"50"` or `"$50"` or `"50 EUR"` |
| `seat_price` = 20 | `extract_float` | Field is `str`, so we parse it |
| `tire` = 20 | `extract_float` | Same — robust against currency symbols |

---

## 💡 A couple of tips

1. **If you want a strict numeric range** for hours instead of a semantic match, you'd need to change the profile so `opening_and_closing` exposes two numeric fields (e.g., `opening_hour: int`, `closing_hour: int`). Then you could write:
   ```yaml
name: opening_hours_check
description: The chatbot reports opening hour 10 and closing hour 20
conversations: 1
oracle: conv[0].opening_hour == 10 and conv[0].closing_hour == 20
```
   That's more precise but requires a profile change. Your call! 😊

2. **If prices can vary slightly** (e.g., taxes, rounding), swap `==` for a tolerance check:
   ```yaml
name: tune_up_price_check
description: The tune-up price should be 50
conversations: 1
oracle: abs(extract_float(conv[0].tune_up) - 50) < 0.01
```

3. **`semantic_content` is LLM-backed**, so allow for tiny variability — but for something as clear as "10 to 20" it should be rock solid.
