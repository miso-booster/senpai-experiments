Great question! 😊 Let me walk you through how I'd build that rule, keeping in mind your `order_id` output field.

## The idea 💡

You want to verify that the chatbot **actually provides an order ID** in the conversation. Since `order_id` is one of your `chatbot.output` fields, the cleanest way is a **single-conversation oracle** that checks the field is present and not empty.

## The rule

```yaml
name: order_id_is_provided
description: The chatbot must provide a non-empty order_id in the conversation
conversations: 1
oracle: conv[0].order_id is not None and len(str(conv[0].order_id).strip()) > 0
```

## Why this works 🔍

- **`conversations: 1`** — this is a simple oracle over a single conversation, no metamorphic pairing needed.
- **`conv[0].order_id`** — pulls the value your profile exposes in `chatbot.output` under the name `order_id`.
- **`is not None`** — guards against the slot never being filled.
- **`len(str(...).strip()) > 0`** — catches the sneaky case where the field exists but is an empty string or just whitespace.

## A couple of alternatives depending on your goal 🎯

**If you want to use the built-in slot helpers** (cleaner, and consistent with the rest of your project):

```yaml
name: order_id_is_provided
description: The chatbot must collect and expose the order_id slot
conversations: 1
oracle: |
  'order_id' not in missing_slots()
```

This reads nicely: *"`order_id` is not among the slots still missing."* ✅

**If you also want to sanity-check the format** (e.g., it looks like an actual ID and not a placeholder):

```yaml
name: order_id_is_provided
description: The chatbot must provide a plausible, non-empty order_id
conversations: 1
oracle: conv[0].order_id is not None and len(str(conv[0].order_id).strip()) > 2
```
