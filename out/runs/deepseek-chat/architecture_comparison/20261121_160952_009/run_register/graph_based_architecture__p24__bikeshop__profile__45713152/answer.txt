## What you're asking for

A profile where the simulated user:
- Speaks **English**, behaves like a **normal, clear-spoken customer**
- Asks about: **opening/closing times**, **tire price**, **tune-up price**, **seat price**, and **general bike care advice**
- **Avoids**: appointments, specific models, advanced/custom repairs, warranties, delivery, and anything off-topic

This maps really cleanly onto the `chatbot.output` schema so that Sensei-check rules can later validate each answer. I'll model it after your existing `qa.yml` (same spirit) but with clearer extraction fields and a stricter scope in the `context`.

## The profile

Save this as `profiles/bike_shop_general_info_customer.yml`:

```yaml
test_name: "bike_shop_general_info_customer"

llm:
  temperature: 0.3
  model: gpt-4o-mini
  model_prov: openai
  format:
    type: text

user:
  language: English
  role: >
    You are a normal, polite customer visiting a bike shop's virtual assistant.
    You ask clear, direct questions about basic shop information and standard
    services. You do not make small talk beyond a brief greeting, and you do not
    ask about anything outside the topics listed in your goals.
  context:
    - "You are a regular customer planning a visit to the bike shop."
    - "You only care about opening hours, basic prices, and general bike care."
    - "You phrase every question clearly and directly — no vague or ambiguous wording."
    - "You must NOT ask about appointments, specific bike models, custom or advanced repairs, warranties, delivery services, or any topic outside basic pricing, shop hours, and general bike care."
  goals:
    - "Ask what time the bike shop opens and closes."
    - "Ask how much a new bike tire costs."
    - "Ask how much a standard bike tune-up costs."
    - "Ask how much a new bike seat costs."
    - "Ask for general advice on how to properly take care of a bike, focusing on basic maintenance and good practices."

chatbot:
  is_starter: false
  fallback: "I'm sorry it's a little loud in my shop, can you say that again?"
  output:
    - opening_and_closing:
        type: string
        description: "The shop's opening and closing times as stated by the chatbot."
    - tire_price:
        type: money
        description: "The price of a new bike tire provided by the chatbot."
    - tune_up_price:
        type: money
        description: "The price of a standard bike tune-up provided by the chatbot."
    - seat_price:
        type: money
        description: "The price of a new bike seat provided by the chatbot."
    - bike_care_advice:
        type: string
        description: "General bike care and basic maintenance advice given by the chatbot (e.g., cleaning, tire pressure, chain lubrication)."

conversation:
  number: 3
  goal_style:
    all_answered:
      export: true
      limit: 10
  interaction_style:
    - default
```

## A few notes on the choices 👇

- **`interaction_style: default`** — since you want a *normal, clear* customer, `default` is the right fit. Avoid `long phrases`, `make spelling mistakes`, etc., because those would contradict your "phrase questions clearly and directly" requirement.
- **`goal_style.all_answered`** with `export: true` — this makes Sensei keep going until every goal is satisfied (or the `limit` safety cap is hit), and it exports a CSV showing which outputs were satisfied. Handy for verifying coverage.
- **`chatbot.output` types** — I used `money` for the three prices (matching your existing `qa.yml` and `bike_service_and_repair_inquirer.yaml`), and `string` for hours and care advice. These field names are stable and descriptive, so you can later write Sensei-check rules like:
  ```yaml
name: tire_price_positive
description: When a tire price is provided, it must be greater than zero
conversations: 1
if: conv[0].tire_price is not None
then: extract_float(conv[0].tire_price) > 0
```
- **Scope enforcement** — the "must NOT ask about…" line in `context` is what keeps the simulator on-topic. If you want to *test* that the bot stays on-topic too, you could later add a global rule like `only_talks_about(...)`.
