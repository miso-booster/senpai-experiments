Hey there! 😊 Happy to help you build a profile for testing appointment scheduling around wheel changes — and I love that you want to stress-test the date/time handling with several variables. Let me put together a clean profile for you 👇

```yaml
test_name: "wheel_change_appointment_scheduler"

llm:
  temperature: 0.6
  model: gpt-4o-mini
  model_prov: openai
  format:
    type: text

user:
  language: English
  role: >
    You are a customer who needs to book an appointment at a bike shop to have
    your wheels changed. You want to confirm the shop can do the wheel change,
    agree on a date and time, and make sure the appointment is properly confirmed.
  context:
    - "Your current wheels are worn out and you want them replaced as soon as possible."
    - "You are flexible but you have a few preferred days and times in mind."
    - "You want the appointment confirmed clearly with the exact date and time."
  goals:
    - "Ask if the shop can change the wheels on your bike."
    - "Request an appointment for {{appointment_date}} at {{appointment_time}} to have your wheels changed."
    - "Confirm the appointment details (date, time, and service) with the assistant."
    - appointment_date:
        function: forward()
        type: string
        data:
          - tomorrow
          - "next Monday"
          - "November 6th, 2024"
          - "the day after tomorrow"
          - "this Saturday"
    - appointment_time:
        function: forward(appointment_date)
        type: string
        data:
          - "09:00"
          - "11:30"
          - "14:00"
          - "16:45"
          - "18:15"
    - wheel_type:
        function: pairwise()
        type: string
        data:
          - front wheel
          - rear wheel
          - both wheels

chatbot:
  is_starter: false
  fallback: "I'm sorry it's a little loud in my shop, can you say that again?"
  output:
    - confirmed_date:
        type: date
        description: "The date of the wheel-change appointment, as confirmed by the assistant."
    - confirmed_time:
        type: time("hh:mm")
        description: "The time of the wheel-change appointment, as confirmed by the assistant."
    - confirmed_service:
        type: string
        description: "The service to be performed (wheel change), as confirmed by the assistant."
    - appointment_confirmation:
        type: string
        description: "The full confirmation message from the assistant, including date, time, and service."

conversation:
  number: combinations(0.6, forward)
  goal_style:
    all_answered:
      export: true
      limit: 12
  interaction_style:
    - random:
      - single questions
      - make spelling mistakes
      - change your mind
  max_cost: 0.5
```

A few notes so you get the most out of it 💡:

- **Date/time coverage:** `appointment_date` uses `forward()` and `appointment_time` nests on it with `forward(appointment_date)`, so you get a nice grid of day × time combinations — perfect for probing how the bot handles different dates and times.
- **`combinations(0.6, forward)`** uses 60% of the forward-generated matrix (you need at least two nested `forward()` variables for this — which you have ✅). Bump it to `combinations` if you want the full grid.
- **`wheel_type` with `pairwise()`** adds variety (front/rear/both) without exploding the case count.
- **`chatbot.output`** exposes `confirmed_date`, `confirmed_time`, `confirmed_service`, and `appointment_confirmation` — these are exactly the fields you'd later reference in Sensei-check rules like `conv[0].confirmed_date` or `conv[0].confirmed_time` if you want to validate date/time handling metamorphically. 🎯
- **`change your mind`** in the interaction style is a nice touch here — it tests whether the bot correctly updates the appointment when you switch dates mid-conversation.
