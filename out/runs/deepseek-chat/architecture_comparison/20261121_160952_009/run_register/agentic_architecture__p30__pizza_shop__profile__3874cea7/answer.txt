Here's your pizza shop profile 🍕 — a rude, impatient customer with 4 goals and 4 variables, two of which use `pairwise()` so the conversation count comes from the pairwise matrix.

```yaml
test_name: "pizza_shop_rude_impatient_order"

llm:
  temperature: 0.7
  model: gpt-4o-mini
  format:
    type: text

user:
  language: English
  role: >
    You are a rude, impatient customer ordering pizza for pickup. You are in a
    hurry, you have no patience for small talk or slow answers, and you expect
    everything to be done fast. You speak bluntly, interrupt, and push the bot
    to move things along quickly.
  context:
    - personality: personalities/rude-user
    - "You are extremely impatient and want your order placed as fast as possible."
    - "You get annoyed if the bot asks unnecessary questions or repeats itself."
    - "You are picking the order up on your way home and are short on time."
  goals:
    - "Demand to know what {{pizza_type}} pizzas are available, and tell them to hurry up."
    - "Order a {{pizza_size}} {{pizza_type}} pizza and snap at them to be quick about it."
    - "Add {{topping}} as a topping and get irritated if they hesitate."
    - "Order {{number_of_drinks}} drinks and demand the total price and how fast it will be ready."
    - pizza_type:
        function: pairwise()
        type: string
        data:
          - Margherita
          - Pepperoni
          - Hawaiian
          - Four cheese
          - Vegetarian
          - Carbonara
    - pizza_size:
        function: pairwise()
        type: string
        data:
          - small
          - medium
          - large
          - extra large
    - topping:
        function: forward()
        type: string
        data:
          - extra cheese
          - mushrooms
          - olives
          - ham
          - jalapenos
    - number_of_drinks:
        function: forward()
        type: string
        data:
          - One
          - Two
          - Three
          - Four

chatbot:
  is_starter: false
  fallback: "I'm sorry, I did not get what you said. I can help you order a pizza and drinks for pickup."
  output:
    - order_summary:
        type: string
        description: "A summary of the pizza order including pizza type, size, topping, and number of drinks. Look for phrases like 'Your order is' or 'You have ordered'."
    - total_price:
        type: currency
        description: "The total price of the order including pizza and drinks. Look for currency symbols like '$' or '€'."
    - estimated_ready_time:
        type: time("hh:mm")
        description: "The estimated time until the order is ready for pickup. Look for phrases like 'ready in' or 'ready by'."
    - order_id:
        type: string
        description: "A unique identifier for the order. Look for phrases like 'order number' or 'order ID'."

conversation:
  number: combinations(1, pairwise)
  max_cost: 1.2
  goal_style:
    all_answered:
      export: false
      limit: 24
  interaction_style:
    - change your mind
    - single questions
```

### What's going on here

**Rude & fast persona** 🏃
- `role` + `context` make the simulated user blunt and impatient (and it reuses your existing `personalities/rude-user` snippet, matching `pizza_and_drink_order_placement.yaml`).
- `interaction_style` uses `single questions` (fires one goal per turn, keeping it snappy) and `change your mind` (adds realistic friction — great for testing how the bot handles a pushy customer).

**4 goals / 4 variables**
- Goals: ask about pizzas → order a pizza → add a topping → order drinks + demand price/ETA.
- Variables: `pizza_type`, `pizza_size`, `topping`, `number_of_drinks`.

**Pairwise → conversation number** 🔢
- `pizza_type` and `pizza_size` both use `pairwise()`. Per the docs, a minimum of **two pairwise variables** is required to derive `conversation.number` from a combination matrix.
- `conversation.number: combinations(1, pairwise)` therefore uses the **full pairwise matrix** generated by those two variables (every `pizza_type` × `pizza_size` pair covered at least once). With 6 types × 4 sizes, that's the pairwise coverage set — not the full 24-case Cartesian product.
- `topping` and `number_of_drinks` use `forward()` so they iterate deterministically without inflating the matrix.

**Outputs for future rules**
- `order_summary`, `total_price`, `estimated_ready_time`, `order_id` are exposed in `chatbot.output`, so you can later write profile-bound rules referencing `conv[i].total_price`, `conv[i].order_id`, etc.
