Hey there! 😊 Happy to help you build that profile — testing date/time handling is a great idea, and Sensei's `forward()`/`another()` functions are perfect for covering different days and times. Let me walk you through it 👇

## 🚲 Profile: `wheel_change_appointment.yml`

```yaml
test_name: "wheel_change_appointment"

llm:
  temperature: 0.5
  model: gpt-4o-mini
  model_prov: openai
  format:
    type: text

user:
  language: English
  role: >
    You are a customer who wants to book an appointment at a bike shop
    to have the wheels on your bike changed. You are friendly but direct,
    and you care about getting a specific day and time confirmed.
  context:
    - "Your current wheels are worn out and you want them replaced with new ones."
    - "You rely on your bike for daily commuting, so you want the appointment soon."
    - "You are flexible on the exact day but you have a preferred time in mind."
  goals:
    - "Ask to book an appointment to have the wheels of your bike changed."
    - "Request the appointment for {{appointment_date}} at {{appointment_time}}."
    - "Confirm the date and time the shop offers you."
    - appointment_date:
        function: forward()
        type: string
        data:
          - tomorrow
          - "next Monday"
          - "November 6th, 2024"
          - "the day after tomorrow"
          - "next Saturday"
    - appointment_time:
        function: another()
        type: string
        data:
          - "09:00"
          - "10:30"
          - "13:00"
          - "15:30"
          - "17:00"

chatbot:
  is_starter: false
  fallback: "I'm sorry it's a little loud in my shop, can you say that again?"
  output:
    - confirmed_date:
        type: date
        description: "The date of the wheel-change appointment confirmed by the assistant."
    - confirmed_time:
        type: time("hh:mm")
        description: "The time of the appointment confirmed by the assistant."
    - confirmed_service:
        type: string
        description: "The service to be performed (wheel change / wheel replacement), as confirmed by the assistant."
    - appointment_confirmation:
        type: string
        description: "The full confirmation message from the chatbot, including date and time."

conversation:
  number: combinations(1, forward)
  goal_style:
    all_answered:
      export: true
      limit: 8
  interaction_style:
    - default
```

## 🧠 Why it's built this way

- **`appointment_date` uses `forward()`** → it walks deterministically through every date, so you get full coverage of each day you care about. 🗓️
- **`appointment_time` uses `another()`** → it cycles through times without repeating until the pool is exhausted, so you don't hammer the same slot over and over. ⏰
- **`conversation.number: combinations(1, forward)`** → since you only have one `forward()` variable, this uses the full forward matrix (all dates). If you want *every date × every time* combination, swap `appointment_time` to `forward()` too and use `combinations(1, forward)` — that gives you the full grid. 🔁
- **`goal_style: all_answered` with `export: true`** → you'll get a CSV showing which outputs were satisfied, super handy for spotting date/time mismatches. 📊
- **`chatbot.output`** captures `confirmed_date` and `confirmed_time` as proper `date`/`time` types — so later Sensei-check rules can compare them cleanly (e.g., `conv[0].confirmed_date`). ✅

## 💡 Quick tip

If you want to stress-test the bot's date *parsing* even more, you can mix in relative and absolute formats in `appointment_date` (like I did: `tomorrow`, `next Monday`, `November 6th, 2024`). That's a great way to see whether the chatbot normalizes them correctly. 😎
