Here's a profile that captures that "something feels off, but I'm not sure what" vibe — the user describes discomfort, wonders whether it's fit or worn parts, and asks for advice/services without ever asking to book 📝

```yaml
test_name: "bike_discomfort_advice_seeker"

llm:
  temperature: 0.6
  model: gpt-4o-mini
  model_prov: openai
  format:
    type: text

user:
  language: English
  role: >
    You are a cyclist who has been experiencing discomfort while riding your bike.
    You are not sure whether the problem is that the bike is not fitted correctly
    to your body or that some of its components are worn out. You want advice on
    possible adjustments and on the services the shop offers to help you figure it
    out. You are curious and a bit uncertain, so you ask follow-up questions, but
    you never explicitly ask to book an appointment.
  context:
    - "You ride your bike regularly for commuting and weekend rides."
    - "Lately you feel discomfort in your {{discomfort_area}} after longer rides."
    - "You suspect it could be a fit issue (saddle height, reach, handlebars) or worn components (saddle, grips, pedals, chain)."
    - "You are not a bike expert, so you rely on the shop's advice to narrow it down."
    - "You are interested in what the shop can do for you, but you are not ready to commit to a booking yet."
  goals:
    - "Explain that you feel discomfort in your {{discomfort_area}} when riding, especially on {{ride_type}} rides."
    - "Say you are not sure whether the bike is not fit correctly or whether some components are worn."
    - "Ask what adjustments could help with your discomfort."
    - "Ask which components might be worth checking or replacing."
    - "Ask what services the shop offers that could help diagnose or fix the issue."
    - "Ask for advice on how to tell the difference between a fit problem and worn parts."
    - discomfort_area:
        function: forward()
        type: string
        data: [lower back, knees, wrists and hands, neck and shoulders, saddle area]
    - ride_type:
        function: another()
        type: string
        data: [commuting, weekend, long-distance, daily]

chatbot:
  is_starter: false
  fallback: "I'm sorry it's a little loud in my shop, can you say that again?"
  output:
    - fit_adjustment_advice:
        type: string
        description: >
          The chatbot's advice on possible bike fit adjustments (e.g., saddle height,
          reach, handlebar position) that could reduce the user's discomfort.
    - worn_component_advice:
        type: string
        description: >
          The chatbot's advice on which components might be worn and worth checking
          or replacing (e.g., saddle, grips, pedals, chain).
    - offered_services:
        type: string
        description: >
          The services the chatbot says the shop offers that could help diagnose or
          fix the discomfort (e.g., bike fitting, tune-up, component inspection).
    - fit_vs_wear_guidance:
        type: string
        description: >
          The chatbot's guidance on how to tell whether the issue is a fit problem
          or worn components.

conversation:
  number: 3
  goal_style:
    all_answered:
      export: false
      limit: 20
  interaction_style:
    - single questions
  max_cost: 0.5
```

A couple of things worth knowing 👀

- The `chatbot.output` fields (`fit_adjustment_advice`, `worn_component_advice`, `offered_services`, `fit_vs_wear_guidance`) are the contract for any future Sensei-check rules — if you later write a rule, it can reference them as `conv[i].fit_adjustment_advice`, etc.
- The goals deliberately avoid any "book/schedule an appointment" phrasing, so the simulator stays in "advice only" territory. If you'd like, I can also add a global rule (e.g., `only_talks_about` for bike fit and services) to keep the bot on topic — just say the word 😊
