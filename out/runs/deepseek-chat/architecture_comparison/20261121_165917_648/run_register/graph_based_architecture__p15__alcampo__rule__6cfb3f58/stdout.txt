That should be `description:`. Sensei-check expects the exact key name.

### 2. Missing `then` (or `oracle`)
Your rule has `when:` and `if:` — but **no `then:` and no `oracle:`**. Sensei-check needs one of these to know *what to assert*. Right now there's nothing to check, so the rule is incomplete.

Also, a subtle point: `if:` is a **precondition** for a metamorphic relation — it must be paired with `then:`. If you only want a plain assertion, use `oracle:` instead.

### 3. Fields must exist in `chatbot.output`
This is the big one ⚠️. Your rule references:
- `conv[0].user_request`
- `conv[1].user_request`
- `conv[0].contact_options`
- `conv[1].customer_service_number`

**All of these must be declared in the target profile's `chatbot.output`.** If the profile doesn't expose `contact_options` or `customer_service_number`, the rule will fail to evaluate. So step one is: check your profile and confirm those slots exist (or add them).

### 4. The logic itself is a bit odd
Your `if:` says *"if both fields are non-null"* — but that's not really a metamorphic relation, and it doesn't assert anything about *correctness* of the contact info. It just says "data was collected." That's more of a `data_collected()`-style check than a real oracle.

## ✅ A working version

Here's a cleaned-up version that actually runs. I'll assume your profile exposes `user_request`, `contact_options`, and `customer_service_number`:

```yaml
name: customer_service_contact_info
description: Ensure the chatbot provides contact options and a customer service number for customer service inquiries.
conversations: 2
when: conv[0].user_request == 'contact options' and conv[1].user_request == 'customer service number'
oracle: conv[0].contact_options is not None and conv[1].customer_service_number is not None
```

### Why this works
- `description` is spelled correctly ✅
- `when:` filters the two comparable conversations ✅
- `oracle:` asserts the actual condition (both fields are populated) ✅
- No dangling `if:` without a `then:` ✅

## 💡 If you want a *stronger* check

If your goal is really "the contact info is **correct**," a plain non-null check is weak. You could use `semantic_content` to verify the content, e.g.:

```yaml
name: customer_service_contact_info
description: Chatbot provides valid contact options and a customer service number.
conversations: 2
when: conv[0].user_request == 'contact options' and conv[1].user_request == 'customer service number'
oracle: >
  semantic_content(conv[0].contact_options, 'phone, email, or chat support options') and
  semantic_content(conv[1].customer_service_number, 'a valid customer service phone number')
```

That actually checks the *meaning* of the answer, not just that a slot got filled 😊
