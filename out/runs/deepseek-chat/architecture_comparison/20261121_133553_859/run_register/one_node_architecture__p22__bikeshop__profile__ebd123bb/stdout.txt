Here's a profile for that scenario — a rider who's uncomfortable on the bike, unsure whether it's fit or worn components, and looking for advice without explicitly booking anything 😊

```yaml
test_name: bike_discomfort_advice_seeker

llm:
  temperature: 0.6
  model: gpt-4o-mini
  model_prov: openai
  format:
    type: text

user:
  language: English
  role: >
    You are a cyclist who has been experiencing discomfort while riding your bike.
    You are not sure whether the bike is not fitted correctly to your body or whether
    some of your components are simply worn out. You want advice from the bike shop
    on possible adjustments or services they offer, but you do NOT explicitly ask to
    book an appointment — you are still exploring your options.
  context:
    - "You ride regularly, mostly for commuting and weekend rides."
    - "Lately you've noticed knee pain and numbness in your hands after longer rides."
    - "You suspect either the saddle height/handlebar position is off, or that your saddle, grips, or drivetrain are worn."
    - "You are friendly but a bit unsure about bike mechanics, so you appreciate clear explanations."
    - "You are not ready to commit to a booking yet — you just want to understand what could be wrong and what the shop can do."
  goals:
    - "Explain that you feel discomfort when riding and you're not sure if it's a fit issue or worn components."
    - "Ask what kinds of adjustments the shop can make to improve comfort (e.g., saddle height, handlebar position)."
    - "Ask whether worn components like the saddle, grips, or drivetrain could be causing the discomfort."
    - "Ask what services the shop offers that could help (e.g., bike fitting, tune-up, component replacement)."
    - "Ask for a rough idea of what such a service would involve or cost, without requesting to book anything."
    - discomfort_area:
        function: forward()
        type: string
        data: [knees, hands, lower back, shoulders]
    - suspected_cause:
        function: forward()
        type: string
        data: [bike fit, worn components, both]

chatbot:
  is_starter: false
  fallback: "I'm sorry it's a little loud in my shop, can you say that again?"
  output:
    - discomfort_advice:
        type: string
        description: >
          The chatbot's advice on possible causes of the rider's discomfort, covering
          both bike fit adjustments and worn components.
    - adjustment_options:
        type: string
        description: >
          The adjustments the chatbot suggests or mentions (e.g., saddle height,
          handlebar position, stem length).
    - relevant_services:
        type: string
        description: >
          The services the shop offers that could address the discomfort (e.g., bike
          fitting, tune-up, component replacement).
    - service_cost_info:
        type: money
        description: >
          Any price or cost estimate the chatbot provides for a relevant service.
          Leave empty if no price is given.

conversation:
  number: 3
  goal_style:
    all_answered:
      export: false
      limit: 20
  interaction_style:
    - single questions
  max_cost: 0.5
```

A couple of notes on the design choices 👀

- **No booking goal** — the goals deliberately stop at asking about services and cost, so the simulator won't push for an appointment. That keeps this profile cleanly separate from your `appointment.yml` and `bike_repair_and_maintenance_scheduler.yaml` profiles.
- **Two `forward()` variables** (`discomfort_area` and `suspected_cause`) give you a small coverage grid across conversations, so the bot gets tested with different complaints.
- **`chatbot.output` fields** are named to be rule-friendly later — e.g., you could write a global rule like `data_collected() == True` or a profile-bound rule checking that `relevant_services` is non-empty.
