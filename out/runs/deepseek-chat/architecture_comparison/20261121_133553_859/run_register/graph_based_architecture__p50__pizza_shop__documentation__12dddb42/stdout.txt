## What is an oracle?

In testing, an **oracle** is simply the **mechanism that tells you whether a test passed or failed** — it's the "source of truth" you compare against. Think of it like a judge at a contest: it looks at the result and decides "correct" or "incorrect." 🧑‍⚖️

## How it works in Sensei-check

In Sensei-check rules, `oracle:` is a field where you write a **Python expression** that must evaluate to `True` (pass) or `False` (fail). It's the condition checked over one or more conversations.

Here's a simple example from your own project:

```yaml
name: only_talks_about_pizzas
description: The chatbot only talks about pizzas
conversations: 1
oracle: 'only_talks_about(''pizza orders, drink orders, information on delivery of pizzas, information about the pizza shop'')'
```

Here the oracle is `only_talks_about(...)` — if the chatbot stays on topic, it returns `True` and the rule passes. ✅

## The "oracle problem" 🧩

The tricky part is that for chatbots, it's often **hard to know the "correct" answer** in advance (there are many valid ways to phrase a reply). That's exactly why Sensei-check supports **metamorphic rules** — instead of checking an absolute answer, you check a **relationship** between conversations.

In those cases, you often use `if:` / `then:` instead of a single `oracle:`. For example, from your project:

```yaml
name: more_toppings_cost_more
conversations: 2
when: conv[0].size == conv[1].size and conv[0].drink == conv[1].drink
if:   len(conv[0].toppings) > len(conv[1].toppings)
then: extract_float(conv[0].price) > extract_float(conv[1].price)
```

Here the `if`/`then` pair *is* the oracle — it checks that "more toppings ⇒ higher price," without needing to know the exact price. 🍕

## Quick summary

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Oracle** | The pass/fail check for a rule |
| **`oracle:` field** | A single boolean expression (single-conversation or global rules) |
| **`if:` / `then:`** | An implication-style oracle, great for metamorphic rules |
