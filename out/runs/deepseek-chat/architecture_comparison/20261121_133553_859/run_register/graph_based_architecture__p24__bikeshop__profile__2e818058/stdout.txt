## What your request maps to

Your description is essentially a clean, well-scoped version of the existing `qa.yml` in your project, but with a few important refinements:

- **Explicit scope guardrails** in `context` (no appointments, models, warranties, etc.)
- **`chatbot.output` fields** that let you validate each answer (hours, tire price, tune-up price, seat price, care advice)
- **`all_answered` goal style** so the conversation only ends once every question has been addressed — perfect for a "does the bot answer everything correctly?" test

## Suggested profile

Save this as `profiles/bike_shop_general_info_and_services.yml`:

```yaml
test_name: bike_shop_general_info_and_services

llm:
  temperature: 0.3
  model: gpt-4o-mini
  model_prov: openai
  format:
    type: text

user:
  language: English
  role: >
    You are a normal, polite customer visiting a bike shop's virtual assistant.
    You want clear, direct answers about the shop's basic information and standard
    services. Phrase your questions clearly and directly, like a regular customer would.
  context:
    - "You are looking for general information about the shop and its standard services."
    - "You only care about: opening/closing times, the price of a new bike tire, the price of a standard tune-up, the price of a new bike seat, and general bike care advice."
    - "Do NOT ask about appointments, specific bike models, custom or advanced repairs, warranties, delivery services, or anything outside basic pricing, shop hours, and general bike care."
    - "You expect the assistant to use proper bike-shop terminology and to state prices clearly."
    - "You do not want unsolicited suggestions about unrelated services."
  goals:
    - "Ask about the opening and closing times of the bike shop."
    - "Ask for the price of a new bike tire."
    - "Ask for the price of a standard bike tune-up."
    - "Ask for the price of a new bike seat."
    - "Ask for general advice on how to properly take care of a bike, focusing on basic maintenance and good practices rather than advanced repairs."

chatbot:
  is_starter: false
  fallback: "I'm sorry it's a little loud in my shop, can you say that again?"
  output:
    - opening_and_closing:
        type: string
        description: "The shop's opening and closing times as stated by the chatbot."
    - tire_price:
        type: money
        description: "The price of a new bike tire provided by the chatbot."
    - tune_up_price:
        type: money
        description: "The price of a standard bike tune-up provided by the chatbot."
    - seat_price:
        type: money
        description: "The price of a new bike seat provided by the chatbot."
    - bike_care_advice:
        type: string
        description: "General bike care and basic maintenance advice given by the chatbot (cleaning, tire pressure, chain lubrication, etc.)."

conversation:
  number: 3
  goal_style:
    all_answered:
      export: true
      limit: 10
  interaction_style:
    - default
```

## Why each piece is there 👇

- **`user.context`** — this is where the "don't ask about X" guardrails live. Sensei uses these as behavioural constraints for the simulated user, so the bot won't drift into appointments or warranties.
- **`user.goals`** — five plain-string goals, one per question. No variables needed here since you want a fixed, repeatable script.
- **`chatbot.output`** — one field per expected answer. `money` is a great fit for the three prices (it's a custom type already used across your project, e.g. in `qa.yml` and `bike_service_and_repair_inquirer.yaml`). If your project doesn't have a `money` type in `types/`, swap it for `float` or `string`.
- **`goal_style.all_answered` with `export: true`** — the conversation keeps going until every goal is addressed, and you get a CSV showing which outputs were satisfied. That's exactly what you want for "did the bot answer each request correctly?"
- **`interaction_style: default`** — natural, customer-like phrasing. No typos or language switching, since you asked for clear and direct.
- **`number: 3`** — three runs gives you a bit of variety in phrasing without burning budget.
