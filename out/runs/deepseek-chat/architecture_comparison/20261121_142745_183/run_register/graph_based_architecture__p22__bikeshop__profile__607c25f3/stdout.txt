## 🚴 Proposed profile: `bike_discomfort_fit_advice.yaml`

```yaml
test_name: bike_discomfort_fit_advice

llm:
  temperature: 0.6
  model: gpt-4o-mini
  format:
    type: text

user:
  language: English
  role: >
    You are a cyclist who has been experiencing discomfort while riding your bike.
    You are not sure whether the bike is not fitted correctly for you or whether
    some of your components (saddle, handlebars, pedals, drivetrain) are simply worn out.
    You want advice on possible adjustments or services the shop offers to fix this,
    but you do NOT explicitly ask to book an appointment.
  context:
    - "You ride your bike regularly for commuting and weekend rides."
    - "The discomfort is mainly in your lower back, knees, and hands after longer rides."
    - "You are not very technical, so you rely on the shop's expertise to diagnose the issue."
    - "You are open to either a fitting/adjustment service or replacing worn components, but you want to understand the options first."
    - "You are not ready to commit to a booking yet — you just want information and advice."
  goals:
    - "Explain that you feel discomfort while riding and you're not sure if it's a fit issue or worn components."
    - "Ask what could be causing the discomfort (fit vs. worn parts)."
    - "Ask what adjustments the shop can make to improve comfort."
    - "Ask what services the shop offers related to bike fitting or component replacement."
    - "Ask how much a {{service}} would cost."
    - service:
        function: forward()
        type: string
        data: [bike fitting, tune-up, saddle replacement]

chatbot:
  is_starter: false
  fallback: "I'm sorry it's a little loud in my shop, can you say that again?"
  output:
    - discomfort_causes:
        type: string
        description: "The chatbot's explanation of possible causes of discomfort (fit issues vs. worn components)."
    - adjustment_advice:
        type: string
        description: "The adjustments the chatbot suggests to improve riding comfort (e.g., saddle height, handlebar position)."
    - available_services:
        type: string
        description: "The services the shop offers related to bike fitting, tune-ups, or component replacement."
    - service_price:
        type: money
        description: "The price quoted for the requested service ({{service}})."

conversation:
  number: 3
  goal_style:
    all_answered:
      export: false
      limit: 20
  interaction_style:
    - single questions
  max_cost: 0.5
```

---

## 🧠 Why it's built this way

**The role & context do the heavy lifting.** The `role` and `context` make it crystal-clear that the user is *uncertain* (fit vs. worn parts) and *not* asking to book — that's the behavioural nuance you described. The simulator will naturally avoid pushing for an appointment. 🙌

**Goals mirror a real conversation flow.** Notice the goals go from "explain the problem" → "ask about causes" → "ask about adjustments" → "ask about services" → "ask about price." That's a natural diagnostic arc, and it keeps the user from jumping straight to booking.

**`single question` interaction style** keeps each turn focused — great for testing whether the bot handles one inquiry at a time cleanly. If you'd rather have a more free-flowing chat, swap it for `default`.

**`chatbot.output` is rule-friendly.** I named the fields descriptively (`discomfort_causes`, `adjustment_advice`, `available_services`, `service_price`) so that later, Sensei-check rules can reference them as `conv[i].service_price`, etc. The `money` type on `service_price` lets you write numeric rules like `extract_float(conv[0].service_price) > 0`. 💡

**The `{{service}}` variable** uses `forward()` so each conversation walks through a different service (fitting → tune-up → saddle replacement), giving you coverage without exploding the number of runs.

## 🔧 Quick tweaks you might want

- **Want more variety in the discomfort?** Add a variable like `{{body_part}}` with `data: [lower back, knees, hands, neck]` and `function: random()`.
- **Want to test multilingual behaviour?** Add `interaction_style: change language: [Spanish]` — your shop bot seems to support English/Spanish (based on `language_issue.yaml`).
- **Want the bot to be pushed a bit?** You could add a goal like *"Ask if the shop can help you figure out the right fit without committing to anything yet"* — that reinforces the "no booking" intent.
