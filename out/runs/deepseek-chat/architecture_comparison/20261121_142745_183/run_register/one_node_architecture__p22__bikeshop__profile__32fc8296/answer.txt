Here's a profile that captures that "something feels off but I'm not sure what" vibe — the user describes discomfort, wonders whether it's fit or worn parts, and asks for advice/services without ever asking to book 🚲

```yaml
test_name: "bike_discomfort_advice_seeker"

llm:
  temperature: 0.6
  model: gpt-4o-mini
  model_prov: openai
  format:
    type: text

user:
  language: English
  role: >
    You are a cyclist who has been experiencing discomfort while riding your bike.
    You are not sure whether the bike is not fitted correctly to your body or whether
    some of your components are simply worn out. You want advice on possible
    adjustments and on the services the shop offers, but you do NOT explicitly ask
    to book an appointment — you are only gathering information for now.
  context:
    - "You ride regularly (commuting and weekend rides) and the discomfort has been building up over the last few weeks."
    - "You feel it mostly in your {{discomfort_area}} after longer rides."
    - "You are unsure whether the cause is bike fit or worn components, and you say so openly."
    - "You are friendly but a bit uncertain — you ask follow-up questions when the assistant gives vague answers."
    - "You are not ready to commit to a booking; if the assistant offers to schedule something, you politely say you just want information for now."
  goals:
    - "Explain that you feel discomfort while riding and that you are not sure if it is a fit issue or worn components."
    - "Mention that the discomfort is mainly in your {{discomfort_area}}."
    - "Ask what adjustments could help make the bike fit better."
    - "Ask whether worn components (like the {{worn_component}}) could be causing the discomfort."
    - "Ask what services the shop offers that could help with this kind of problem."
    - "Ask how you could tell the difference between a fit problem and a worn-part problem."
    - discomfort_area:
        function: forward()
        type: string
        data: [lower back, knees, wrists and hands, neck and shoulders, saddle area]
    - worn_component:
        function: forward(discomfort_area)
        type: string
        data: [saddle, handlebars, pedals, chain and drivetrain, tires]

chatbot:
  is_starter: false
  fallback: "I'm sorry it's a little loud in my shop, can you say that again?"
  output:
    - fit_adjustment_advice:
        type: string
        description: >
          The advice the chatbot gives about possible bike-fit adjustments
          (e.g., saddle height, saddle position, handlebar reach, stem length,
          cleat position) that could reduce the user's discomfort.
    - worn_component_advice:
        type: string
        description: >
          The chatbot's explanation of whether worn components (saddle, chain,
          drivetrain, tires, etc.) could be causing the discomfort and how to
          check for wear.
    - offered_services:
        type: string
        description: >
          The services the chatbot says the shop offers that are relevant to
          discomfort, fit, or worn components (e.g., bike fitting, tune-up,
          component replacement, inspection).
    - fit_vs_wear_guidance:
        type: string
        description: >
          The chatbot's guidance on how to distinguish a bike-fit problem from
          a worn-component problem.
    - appointment_offer:
        type: string
        description: >
          Any offer by the chatbot to book an appointment or visit the shop.
          The user does not request this, so this field captures whether the
          chatbot proactively suggested it.

conversation:
  number: 3
  goal_style:
    all_answered:
      export: false
      limit: 20
  interaction_style:
    - single question
  max_cost: 0.5
```

A couple of notes so it behaves the way you described 👇

- **No booking goal** — the user never asks to schedule anything, and the `context` explicitly tells the simulator to decline politely if the bot offers to book. The `appointment_offer` output lets you check *whether* the bot pushed a booking anyway (handy for a rule later).
- **`forward()` nesting** — `worn_component` depends on `discomfort_area`, so you get a nice deterministic grid of area/component combos across the 3 conversations.
- **`single question` style** keeps each turn focused, which pairs well with the `all_answered` goal style.
