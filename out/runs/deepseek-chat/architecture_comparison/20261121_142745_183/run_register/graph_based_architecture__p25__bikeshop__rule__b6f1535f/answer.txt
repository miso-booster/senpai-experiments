## The rule 🎯

Since you want to check **fixed expected values** (opening/closing between 10–20, tune-up = 50, seat = 20, tyre = 20), this is actually a **single-conversation oracle** — not a metamorphic rule. You're asserting absolute values, not relations between conversations. That's totally fine and supported! 👍

```yaml
name: bike_shop_prices_and_hours
description: >
  Checks that the bike shop bot reports opening/closing hours between 10 and 20,
  a tune-up price of 50, a seat price of 20, and a tyre price of 20.
conversations: 1
oracle: >
  extract_float(conv[0].opening_and_closing) >= 10
  and extract_float(conv[0].opening_and_closing) <= 20
  and extract_float(conv[0].tune_up) == 50
  and extract_float(conv[0].seat_price) == 20
  and extract_float(conv[0].tire) == 20
on-error: >
  Expected hours in [10,20], tune_up=50, seat=20, tire=20 — got
  opening_and_closing={conv[0].opening_and_closing},
  tune_up={conv[0].tune_up},
  seat_price={conv[0].seat_price},
  tire={conv[0].tire}
```

## ⚠️ Important caveat about `opening_and_closing`

`extract_float` returns **only the first number** it finds in the string. So if the bot says something like:

> "We open at 10 and close at 20"

…then `extract_float` will return `10` — which passes your `>= 10 and <= 20` check, but it **won't actually verify the closing time**. 😬

If you want to be stricter and check **both** the opening and closing values, you'd need to split the string first. Since the DSL doesn't have a built-in "extract all numbers" helper, a practical approach is to check that the string contains both expected values:

```yaml
name: bike_shop_prices_and_hours
description: >
  Checks that the bike shop bot reports opening/closing hours 10 and 20,
  a tune-up price of 50, a seat price of 20, and a tyre price of 20.
conversations: 1
oracle: >
  '10' in conv[0].opening_and_closing
  and '20' in conv[0].opening_and_closing
  and extract_float(conv[0].tune_up) == 50
  and extract_float(conv[0].seat_price) == 20
  and extract_float(conv[0].tire) == 20
on-error: >
  Expected hours 10–20, tune_up=50, seat=20, tire=20 — got
  opening_and_closing={conv[0].opening_and_closing},
  tune_up={conv[0].tune_up},
  seat_price={conv[0].seat_price},
  tire={conv[0].tire}
```

This version is more faithful to your intent: it verifies that **both** `10` and `20` appear in the hours string. 🎯

## A couple of extra tips 💡

1. **`conv[0]` vs bare field names** — In a `conversations: 1` rule, both `conv[0].field` and bare `field` work. I used `conv[0].` for clarity and consistency with the docs.

2. **`on-error` is your friend** — It's an f-string, so `{conv[0].tune_up}` gets interpolated with the actual value. Super handy for debugging failures! 🐛
