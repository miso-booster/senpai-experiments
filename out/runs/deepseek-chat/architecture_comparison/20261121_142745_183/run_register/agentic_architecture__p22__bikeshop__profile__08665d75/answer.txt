### What this profile does and why

- **Role & context** capture the exact scenario: discomfort, uncertainty between **fit vs. worn components**, wanting **advice** but **not** explicitly booking. The `skeptical-user` personality matches the exploratory, cautious tone of your other bike profiles.
- **Goals** walk the user through the natural flow of the conversation — describing the problem, asking about causes, adjustments, services, fit details, wear detection, and a next-step recommendation — while deliberately **never** asking to book.
- **`chatbot.output`** is designed as a clean contract for future SENSEI-check rules (per §2.1 of `sensei-chat-guide.md`). Each field maps to a business property you'd likely want to validate, e.g.:
  - `suggested_adjustments` / `possible_causes` → check the bot actually addresses both fit and wear.
  - `available_services` / `bike_fitting_details` → check the shop's offerings are described.
  - `appointment_offered` → a useful field to test that the bot doesn't push booking when the user hasn't asked (or to check how it handles a proactive offer).
- **Conversation controls** mirror your existing profiles (`number: 3`, `max_cost: 0.5`, `all_answered` with `limit: 30`, `single questions`), so it runs consistently with the rest of the project.

A couple of things worth flagging:

1. **`money` type note:** your other profiles use `type: money` in `chatbot.output`, but the guide (`sensei-chat-guide.md` §6) lists `int`, `float`, `string`, `time`, `date`, and `list[...]` as the built-in output types. I avoided `money` here since this profile doesn't extract prices — but if you later add pricing fields, you may want to confirm whether `money` is a custom type in your `types/` folder.
2. **`appointment_offered`** is intentionally a `string` (not a boolean) so it can capture both whether the bot offered and how the user reacted — handy for a rule later.
