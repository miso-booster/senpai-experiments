Hey there! 😊 Great question — Sensei profiles are pretty flexible, and several sections are optional depending on how much detail you need.

Here’s what you can safely omit (or only partially fill) when you don’t need them:

- **`llm`** section: totally optional. If you skip it, Sensei uses its defaults (like the default model and temperature). Handy when you don’t need custom settings.
- **`user.context`**: those extra background bullet points are optional. Only add them if you want to shape the simulated user’s mindset or tone.
- **Personality references** inside `context`: optional too — they’re just reusable prompt snippets if you want them.
- **`chatbot.fallback`**: recommended but not mandatory. If you leave it out, Sensei doesn’t “invent” one for you; just make sure your real bot has a sensible fallback so conversations don’t get stuck.
- **`chatbot.output`** items: you decide which fields you care about. No minimum count — include only what you plan to extract and validate later.
- **`conversation` controls** like `max_cost`, `interaction_style`, or even `goal_style` variations are configurable but not required unless you need that extra steering. (If you skip the whole `conversation` block, Sensei falls back to defaults: 1 conversation, standard turn limit, etc.)
- Within `user.goals`, only variables referenced with `{{…}}` in your goal strings need a corresponding definition. No unused variables required, and you can keep extra goal lines out if your scenario is simple.
- Any **custom types** under `types/` are optional unless you actually reference them in goals or outputs.

So, the bare minimum is basically: `test_name`, `user.language`, at least one entry in `user.goals`, and usually `chatbot.is_starter`. Everything else you can add as your testing scenario gets more sophisticated.
