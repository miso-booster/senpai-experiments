The `pairwise()` function in Sensei profiles is a smart way to cover a lot of variable combinations without exploding into every possible mix.

### What `pairwise()` does  
It ensures that **every pair of variables** assigned with `pairwise()` appears together at least once across the generated conversations. That gives you broad coverage while keeping the number of runs manageable.

### When to use it  
- You have several options for multiple variables (like size, crust, drink) and want to check interactions between them.
- You care about combinations of two variables at a time, but not necessarily every full Cartesian combo.

### How to set it up  
Declare at least **two variables** in your `user.goals` section that use `function: pairwise()`. Then, in your `conversation.number`, pick something like `combinations` or `combinations(<fraction>, pairwise)` so Sensei knows to build the pairwise matrix:

```yaml
conversation:
  number: combinations      # or combinations(0.6, pairwise)
```

### Important reminders
- Needs **two or more** pairwise variables to work.
- Pairwise coverage focuses on **pairs**—higher-order combos (triplets, etc.) aren’t guaranteed.
- You can mix it with other selection functions (`forward`, `another`, etc.), just remember only the `pairwise()` variables participate in that pair coverage.

So, in short: `pairwise()` gives you a sweet spot between “test everything” and “test too little,” ensuring the most common interaction bugs are caught without overwhelming you with conversations. Let me know if you want a concrete example from your profiles!
