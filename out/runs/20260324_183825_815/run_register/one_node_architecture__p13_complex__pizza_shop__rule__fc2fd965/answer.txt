Here's a quick guide on how to approach the audit:

### 1. Review Profiles and Their Outputs
Start by listing the outputs defined in each profile. For example, in your profiles, you have outputs like `available_information`, `opening_hours`, `total_cost`, etc.

### 2. Review Rules and Their Conditions
Next, look at the rules you have. For instance, the `more_toppings_cost_more` rule checks if adding more toppings increases the price, which relates to the `price` output in the profiles.

### 3. Mapping Rules to Profile Outputs
Create a mapping of which rules validate which outputs. For example:

- **Rule:** `more_toppings_cost_more`
  - **Validates Outputs:** `price` from profiles related to pizza orders.

### 4. Identify Gaps
After mapping, check if there are outputs in your profiles that do not have corresponding rules to validate them. For instance, if you have an output like `estimated_order_time` but no rules checking its accuracy or consistency, that would be a gap.

### Example Mapping
Here’s a simplified example of how you might structure your findings:

| Profile Output                     | Related Rule(s)                     | Coverage Status |
|------------------------------------|-------------------------------------|------------------|
| `available_information`            | `only_talks_about_pizza`           | Covered           |

| `total_cost`                       | `more_toppings_cost_more`           | Covered           |
| `estimated_order_time`             | (No related rules)                  | Gap               |

### Conclusion
Once you’ve completed this mapping, you’ll have a clearer picture of your coverage and can prioritize creating new rules to fill in the gaps.
