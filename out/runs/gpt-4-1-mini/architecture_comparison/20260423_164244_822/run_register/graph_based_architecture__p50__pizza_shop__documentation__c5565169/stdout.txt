Hey! Great question 😊 In the context of **SENSEI Rules** (especially Sensei-check), an **oracle** is basically a condition or a test that tells you whether a conversation or a set of conversations behaves correctly.

Think of it like a "truth test" or a "check" that the chatbot’s output must pass. It’s a logical expression written in the Sensei-check DSL (a YAML-based language) that inspects fields extracted from chatbot conversations to verify if the bot is doing what it should.

### More simply:
- An **oracle** answers: *Did the chatbot behave as expected in this conversation?*
- It can be applied to **one conversation** (single-conversation oracle) or to **multiple conversations** (metamorphic oracle).
- If the oracle condition fails, it means the chatbot might have a bug or unexpected behavior.

---

### Example of a single-conversation oracle:

```yaml
name: small_base_price_floor
description: Small pizzas should cost at least $10
conversations: 1
when: size == 'small'
oracle: extract_float(price) >= 10 and currency(price) == 'USD'
```

Here, the oracle checks that for a small pizza order, the price extracted from the chatbot’s output is at least $10 and in USD.

---

### Example of a metamorphic oracle (comparing two conversations):

```yaml
name: toppings_monotonicity
description: More toppings increases total price
conversations: 2
when: conv[0].size == conv[1].size and conv[0].drink == conv[1].drink and conv[0].number == conv[1].number
if:   len(conv[0].toppings) > len(conv[1].toppings)
then: extract_float(conv[0].price) > extract_float(conv[1].price)
```

This oracle says: *If two orders have the same size, drink, and quantity, then the one with more toppings should cost more.*

---

So, in short: **an oracle is the rule or formula that checks if the chatbot’s output is correct or consistent.** It’s the heart of automated testing in Sensei-check!
