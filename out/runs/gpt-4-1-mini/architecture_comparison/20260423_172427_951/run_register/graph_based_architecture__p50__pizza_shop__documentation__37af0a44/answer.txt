Hey! Great question 😊 In the context of **SENSEI Rules** (especially in Sensei-check), an **oracle** is basically a condition or a test that tells you whether a chatbot’s behavior or output is correct for a given conversation or set of conversations.

Think of it like a “truth test” or a “check” that you write to verify if the chatbot did what it was supposed to do.

---

### More specifically:

- An **oracle** is a boolean expression (true/false) written in the Sensei-check DSL (YAML + Python-like expressions).
- It inspects the chatbot’s outputs (like prices, order IDs, languages, etc.) extracted from conversations.
- If the oracle evaluates to **true**, the chatbot passed that test.
- If it’s **false**, it means the chatbot’s response violated the expected behavior.

---

### Types of oracles in Sensei-check:

1. **Single-conversation oracle:**  
   Checks conditions on one conversation’s output.  
   Example: "The price should be at least $10."

2. **Metamorphic oracle:**  
   Compares two or more conversations to check relations between them.  
   Example: "If one pizza has more toppings than another (same size), its price should be higher."

3. **Global oracle:**  
   Checks properties across all conversations, like uniqueness of order IDs.

---

### Example of a single-conversation oracle:

```yaml
name: small_base_price_floor
description: Small pizzas should cost at least $10
conversations: 1
when: size == 'small'
oracle: extract_float(price) >= 10 and currency(price) == 'USD'
```

Here, the oracle checks that for small pizzas, the extracted price is at least $10 in USD.

---

### In short:

> An **oracle** is your *automated test* that says: “Is this chatbot output valid or not?” It’s the key to verifying chatbot correctness in Sensei.
