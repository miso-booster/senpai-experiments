Testing how a chatbot handles dates and times is a fantastic use case. To do this efficiently, we can use the `pairwise()` function in our variables. This will mix and match different date formats (like "tomorrow" vs. a specific date) and time formats (like "09:00" vs. "5 PM") to ensure your bot understands all of them without having to run a massive, exhaustive list of every single combination.

Here is a ready-to-use profile YAML for your wheel change appointment test:

```yaml
test_name: "wheel_change_appointment_test"

llm:
  temperature: 0.5
  model: gpt-4o
  model_prov: openai
  format:
    type: text

user:
  language: English
  role: >
    You are a cyclist whose bike wheels are damaged. You need to schedule a 
    service appointment at the local bike shop to get them changed.
  context:
    - "You want to make sure the shop can accommodate your specific schedule."
    - "You are polite but focused on getting your appointment booked."
  goals:
    - "Ask to schedule an appointment to change your wheels on {{appointment_date}} at {{appointment_time}}."
    - appointment_date:
        function: pairwise()
        type: string
        data: ["tomorrow", "next Monday", "November 20th", "Friday"]
    - appointment_time:
        function: pairwise()
        type: string
        data: ["09:00", "12:30 PM", "15:00", "5 PM"]

chatbot:
  is_starter: false
  fallback: "I'm sorry, it's a little loud in the shop. Could you repeat that?"
  output:
    - confirmed_date:
        type: date("yyyy-mm-dd")
        description: "The final date confirmed by the chatbot for the appointment."
    - confirmed_time:
        type: time("hh:mm")
        description: "The final time confirmed by the chatbot for the appointment."
    - service_booked:
        type: string
        description: "The type of service booked (e.g., wheel change or tire replacement)."

conversation:
  number: combinations(1, pairwise)
  goal_style:
    all_answered:
      export: true
      limit: 15
  interaction_style:
    - random:
      - default
      - change your mind
```

### 🧐 Let's break down the magic happening here:

1. **The Variables (`appointment_date` & `appointment_time`)**: 
   By using `pairwise()` on both variables, Sensei will automatically generate a smart matrix of conversations. It guarantees that every date is paired with every time at least once across the test suite, giving you great coverage!
2. **The Output Extraction (`chatbot.output`)**: 
   At the end of the conversation, Sensei will extract the `confirmed_date` and `confirmed_time`. Notice how we used `date("yyyy-mm-dd")` and `time("hh:mm")`? This tells the extractor exactly how you want the data formatted, making it super easy to write **Sensei Rules** later to verify if the bot booked the *correct* time! ⏱️
3. **The Interaction Style (`change your mind`)**:
   I sneaked in a random `change your mind` interaction style! 👀 This means in some conversations, the simulated user might say, *"Actually, can we do Tuesday instead?"* This is the ultimate stress test for a booking chatbot!
4. **Conversation Number (`combinations(1, pairwise)`)**:
   This tells Sensei to run 100% (`1`) of the combinations generated by our `pairwise` functions.

Just save this as `wheel_change_appointment.yml` inside your `profiles/` folder, and you're ready to roll! 🛞💨
