Hi there! 😊 I'd be happy to explain what a metamorphic rule is in Sensei!

In traditional testing, you usually check if a system's output matches an exact expected answer (like, *"Is the final price exactly $15?"*). But with chatbots, answers can vary a lot in natural language, making it really tricky to predict the exact response. This is known as the "oracle problem."

A **metamorphic rule** in Sensei-check solves this by checking the *relationship* between two or more conversations, rather than looking for one absolute answer. 

**Here is a simple everyday analogy 🍕:**
Imagine you chat with a bot and order a medium pizza with 2 toppings. Then, in a separate conversation, you order the *exact same* medium pizza but with 4 toppings. Even if you don't know the exact prices of the pizzas, you know for a fact that the second pizza should cost *more* than the first one. 

That logical relationship is a metamorphic rule! 

In Sensei, you write these rules in YAML. A typical metamorphic rule defines:
1. **How many conversations** it needs to compare (usually 2 or more).
2. **A filter (`when`)** to select comparable conversations (e.g., *when the pizza size and drinks are the exact same*).
3. **The relation (`if` / `then`)** that must hold true (e.g., *if conversation A has more toppings than conversation B, then conversation A's price must be higher*).

By comparing conversations this way, you can test if your chatbot's logic is sound across hundreds of different scenarios without having to hardcode every single expected price or answer.
