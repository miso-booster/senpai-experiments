I have loaded the SENSEI Profiles documentation (`sensei-chat-guide.md`) to ensure the profile is structured correctly. 

Here is the YAML profile tailored to your scenario. It simulates a cyclist experiencing discomfort who wants advice on whether it's a bike fit issue or worn components, without explicitly asking for an appointment.

```yaml
test_name: "cyclist_discomfort_advice"

llm:
  temperature: 0.7
  model: gpt-4o
  model_prov: openai
  format:
    type: text

user:
  language: English
  role: >
    You are a cyclist who is experiencing discomfort while riding your bike.
    You are unsure whether the issue is caused by a poor bike fit or worn-out components.
    You want to ask the bike shop for advice on possible adjustments or services they offer to help resolve this issue.
  context:
    - "You feel discomfort in your {{body_part}} after riding for a while."
    - "You do not explicitly ask to book an appointment; you just want advice and to know what services are available."
  goals:
    - "Explain your discomfort in the {{body_part}} and ask if it could be a bike fit issue or worn components."
    - "Ask what adjustments or services the shop offers to help with this."
    - body_part:
        function: random()
        type: string
        data: [lower back, knees, neck, saddle area, wrists]

chatbot:
  is_starter: false
  fallback: "I'm sorry, I didn't quite catch that. Could you rephrase?"
  output:
    - suggested_services:
        type: list[string]
        description: "Services or adjustments suggested by the chatbot (e.g., bike fit, component replacement, suspension tuning)"
    - appointment_offered:
        type: string
        description: "Did the chatbot proactively offer to book an appointment? (Yes/No)"

conversation:
  number: 3
  goal_style:
    all_answered:
      export: true
      limit: 15
  interaction_style:
    - default
```

### 🚲 How this profile works:

1. **Role & Context**: The `role` clearly defines the user's persona as a cyclist seeking advice. The `context` enforces the constraint that the user should **not** explicitly ask to book an appointment, and it introduces a variable `{{body_part}}` to make the conversations feel more natural and varied.
2. **Goals & Variables**: The `goals` section instructs the simulator to explain the discomfort and ask about services. The `body_part` variable uses a `random()` function to pick from a list of common cycling pain points (lower back, knees, neck, etc.) for each conversation.
3. **Chatbot Output**: The `chatbot.output` section extracts two key pieces of information at the end of the conversation:
   - `suggested_services`: A list of the services the chatbot recommended.
   - `appointment_offered`: A string (Yes/No) to track if the chatbot proactively tried to book an appointment, which is great for testing if your bot takes the initiative when a user is hesitant!
4. **Conversation Controls**: It will run `3` conversations using the `all_answered` goal style, meaning the simulator will stop once it has successfully asked about the discomfort and the available services.
