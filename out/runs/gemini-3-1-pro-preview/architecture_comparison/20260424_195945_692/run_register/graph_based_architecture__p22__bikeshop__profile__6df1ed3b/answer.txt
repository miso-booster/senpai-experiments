Hello there! Senpai here! 😊 

Ouch, riding a bike with discomfort is the worst! It’s a very realistic scenario, and it’s a great test case to see how empathetic and helpful your bike shop chatbot can be without being overly pushy about booking an appointment. 🚲🛠️

To make this work perfectly in Sensei, we will use the `user.context` to explicitly tell the simulated user **not** to ask for an appointment, while using `user.goals` to guide them through explaining their pain and asking for advice. 

Here is a complete, ready-to-run profile tailored exactly to your needs. You can save this as `bike_discomfort_advice_seeker.yaml` in your `profiles/` folder:

```yaml
test_name: "bike_discomfort_advice_seeker"

llm:
  temperature: 0.6
  model: gpt-4o-mini
  format:
    type: text

user:
  language: English
  role: >
    A cyclist experiencing physical discomfort while riding, seeking expert advice 
    on whether it's a bike fit issue or worn-out components.
  context:
    - "You feel discomfort in your knees and lower back after riding for more than 30 minutes."
    - "You are unsure if your bike is the wrong size, poorly adjusted, or if parts like the saddle or pedals are just worn out."
    - "You want to know what the shop recommends and what services they offer to help."
    - "CRITICAL: Do NOT explicitly ask to book an appointment. You are only gathering information and advice for now."
  goals:
    - "Explain your riding discomfort and ask if it sounds like a bike fit issue or worn components."
    - "Ask what specific adjustments or checks the shop recommends for this problem."
    - "Inquire about the services the shop offers related to bike fitting or component inspection."
    - "Ask for the estimated cost of these recommended services."

chatbot:
  is_starter: false
  fallback: "I'm sorry it's a little loud in my shop, can you say that again?"
  output:
    - discomfort_diagnosis_advice:
        type: string
        description: "The chatbot's advice on whether the issue is likely related to bike fit or worn components."
    - recommended_services:
        type: string
        description: "The specific services or adjustments recommended by the chatbot to fix the user's discomfort."
    - service_costs:
        type: string
        description: "The estimated costs provided by the chatbot for the recommended services."
    - pushed_appointment:
        type: string
        description: "Did the chatbot try to push or suggest booking an appointment? Extract 'Yes' or 'No' and a brief reason."

conversation:
  number: 3
  max_cost: 0.5
  goal_style:
    all_answered:
      export: false
      limit: 15
  interaction_style:
    - default
```

### 👀 A quick breakdown of why this works so well:

1. **The Negative Constraint:** In the `user.context`, I added a `CRITICAL` instruction telling the LLM *not* to ask for an appointment. Sensei's user simulator is very obedient, so this ensures the user stays purely in "information-gathering" mode.
2. **The `chatbot.output` Contract:** I added a fun field called `pushed_appointment`. Since the user isn't asking for one, this is a fantastic way to test if your chatbot is aggressively trying to close a sale or if it respects the user's pace! You can later write a **Sensei Rule** to check this field.
3. **Interaction Style:** I left it as `default` so the simulated user can naturally weave their story about knee and back pain, making the conversation feel very human.
