Since you want to verify that the assistant returns specific, fixed values (like the tune-up being 50 and the seat being 20), we will create a **single-conversation rule** (also known as a traditional oracle). 

To do this, we'll use the exact field names you defined in your `chatbot.output` (`opening_and_closing`, `tire`, `tune_up`, and `seat_price`). We can use the handy `extract_float()` helper to check the prices, and the `semantic_content()` helper to make sure the opening hours mean "10 to 20" even if the bot phrases it like "10 AM to 8 PM" or "10:00 - 20:00".

Here is the ready-to-use rule for your project:

```yaml
name: verify_bike_shop_info
description: Checks if the bike shop assistant provides the correct hours and prices for the items.
conversations: 1
oracle: >
  semantic_content(opening_and_closing, '10 to 20') and
  extract_float(tune_up) == 50 and
  extract_float(seat_price) == 20 and
  extract_float(tire) == 20
```

### 🧠 A quick breakdown of what's happening here:
* **`conversations: 1`**: We only need to look at one conversation at a time to check these static values.
* **`semantic_content(...)`**: This is a super smart LLM-backed helper! It checks if the text in `opening_and_closing` semantically matches the concept of "10 to 20", so you don't have to worry about exact string matching.
* **`extract_float(...)`**: This helper pulls the first number it finds out of the text. So even if your bot replies with "$50.00" or "50 bucks", it will correctly extract `50` to compare it!

You can save this in your rules folder, and Sensei-check will automatically evaluate your bike shop conversations against it.
