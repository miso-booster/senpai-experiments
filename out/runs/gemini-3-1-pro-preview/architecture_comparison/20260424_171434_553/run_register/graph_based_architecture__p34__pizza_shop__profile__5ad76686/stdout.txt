To create a scenario where the simulated user changes their mind halfway through, we can use a super handy feature in Sensei Profiles called **`interaction_style`**. Specifically, we'll use the **`change your mind`** style! 

When you combine this interaction style with a clear `role` and `context`, the Sensei user simulator will naturally start asking for a custom pizza, hesitate or backtrack, and then switch to a predefined one.

Here is a complete, ready-to-use profile you can save in your `profiles/` folder (for example, as `pizza_order_change_mind.yml`):

```yaml
test_name: "pizza_order_change_mind"

llm:
  temperature: 0.7
  model: gpt-4o-mini
  format:
    type: text

user:
  language: English
  role: >
    You are a customer ordering a pizza. You initially want to build your own custom pizza, 
    but halfway through the conversation, you decide to change your mind and order a predefined pizza instead.
  context:
    - "You are hungry and a bit indecisive."
  goals:
    - "Start by asking to create a custom pizza with cheese, mushrooms, and pepperoni."
    - "Change your mind and order a {{predefined_pizza}} pizza instead."
    - "Ask for the total price of the new order."
    - predefined_pizza:
        function: random()
        type: string
        data: [Margarita, Pepperoni, Hawaiian, Four cheese]

chatbot:
  is_starter: false
  fallback: "I'm sorry, I didn't catch that. Would you like a custom or predefined pizza?"
  output:
    - final_pizza_ordered:
        type: string
        description: "The final pizza that the user decided to order after changing their mind."
    - total_price:
        type: currency
        description: "The final price of the order."

conversation:
  number: 2
  goal_style:
    all_answered:
      limit: 15
  interaction_style:
    - change your mind
```

### 💡 Why this works so well:
1. **`interaction_style: [change your mind]`**: This is a reserved keyword in Sensei. It specifically instructs the user simulator to alter its decisions during the chat. It's perfect for testing if your chatbot can gracefully handle a user clearing their cart or updating their order!
2. **The `role` and `goals`**: We explicitly tell the user persona *what* to change their mind about (from custom to a `{{predefined_pizza}}`).
3. **`chatbot.output`**: I added a `final_pizza_ordered` field. This is a great way to use **Sensei-check rules** later to verify that your chatbot actually registered the predefined pizza and discarded the custom one! 👀
