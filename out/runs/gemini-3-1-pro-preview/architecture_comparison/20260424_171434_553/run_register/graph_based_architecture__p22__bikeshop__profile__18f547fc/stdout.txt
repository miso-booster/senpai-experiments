To make sure our simulated user behaves exactly as you described, we will use the `user.context` section to firmly instruct the LLM **not** to book an appointment. Then, we'll use `user.goals` to guide the user to ask about their discomfort, bike fit, and worn components. 

Here is a complete, ready-to-use Sensei Profile YAML for your scenario:

```yaml
test_name: bike_discomfort_advice_seeker

llm:
  temperature: 0.6
  model: gpt-4o-mini
  format:
    type: text

user:
  language: English
  role: >
    A cyclist experiencing discomfort while riding, seeking advice on whether the issue is bike fit or worn components.
  context:
    - "You feel discomfort when riding your bike, such as back, neck, or knee pain."
    - "You are unsure if the bike fit is wrong or if some components (like the saddle or grips) are simply worn out."
    - "You want to know what adjustments or services the shop offers to help with this."
    - "IMPORTANT: You are just gathering information. Do NOT explicitly ask to book or schedule an appointment."
  goals:
    - "Explain your riding discomfort and ask if it could be due to a poor bike fit or worn components."
    - "Ask what specific adjustments or services the shop offers to resolve riding discomfort."
    - "Inquire about the price of a bike fitting service or a general inspection."

chatbot:
  is_starter: false
  fallback: "I'm sorry it's a little loud in my shop, can you say that again?"
  output:
    - discomfort_advice:
        type: string
        description: "The chatbot's advice regarding whether the discomfort is due to bike fit or worn components."
    - suggested_services:
        type: string
        description: "The services or adjustments suggested by the chatbot to help with the discomfort (e.g., bike fitting, component replacement)."
    - service_prices:
        type: string
        description: "Any pricing information provided by the chatbot for bike fitting or general inspections."

conversation:
  number: 3
  max_cost: 0.5
  goal_style:
    all_answered:
      export: false
      limit: 15
  interaction_style:
    - single questions
```

### 💡 Why we set it up this way:
* **`user.context`**: This is the perfect place to set behavioral boundaries. By adding the `IMPORTANT:` note, we ensure the user simulator knows its boundaries and won't accidentally try to book a time slot.
* **`user.goals`**: We broke the inquiry down into logical steps. First, explaining the problem, then asking about services, and finally asking about prices.
* **`chatbot.output`**: We defined three specific fields (`discomfort_advice`, `suggested_services`, and `service_prices`) so that at the end of the test, you can easily extract and verify if your chatbot gave good, empathetic advice and accurate pricing! 📝
* **`interaction_style`**: I set it to `[single question]` so the user asks things step-by-step, which feels very natural for someone seeking advice.
