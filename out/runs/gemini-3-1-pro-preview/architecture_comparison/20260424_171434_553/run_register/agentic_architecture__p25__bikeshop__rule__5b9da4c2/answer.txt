Based on the SENSEI RULES documentation (`sensei-check-doc.md`), we can create a single-conversation rule (an oracle) that directly checks the extracted fields from your profile's `chatbot.output`.

Here is the rule you need:

```yaml
name: check_bike_shop_prices_and_hours
description: Checks that the opening hours are 10 to 20, tune-up is 50, seat is 20, and tire is 20.
conversations: 1
oracle: |
  semantic_content(opening_and_closing, '10 to 20') and extract_float(tune_up) == 50 and extract_float(seat_price) == 20 and extract_float(tire) == 20
```

### How this rule works with your profile:
1. **Direct Field Mapping**: The variables in the rule (`opening_and_closing`, `tune_up`, `seat_price`, and `tire`) perfectly match the names of the slots you defined in your profile's `chatbot.output`.
2. **`semantic_content` helper**: Since `opening_and_closing` is a string that might be formatted in various ways by the LLM (e.g., "from 10am to 8pm", "10:00 - 20:00"), we use the `semantic_content()` helper. This uses an LLM under the hood to robustly verify if the extracted text semantically means "10 to 20".
3. **`extract_float` helper**: For the prices (`tune_up`, `seat_price`, and `tire`), we use the `extract_float()` helper. This safely pulls the first numeric value out of the extracted strings or money types (e.g., turning "$50.00" or "50 USD" into just `50.0`), allowing us to do a strict mathematical comparison. 

You can save this YAML into your `rules` directory, and SENSEI will automatically validate these constraints against the conversations generated by your bike shop profile! Let me know if you need any adjustments. 😊
