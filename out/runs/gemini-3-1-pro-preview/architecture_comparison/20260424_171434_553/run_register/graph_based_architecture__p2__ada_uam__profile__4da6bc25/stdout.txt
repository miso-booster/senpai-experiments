Creating a profile for a newly arrived Erasmus student is a fantastic use case! It’s super important to make sure your chatbot can provide clear, step-by-step support to someone who might be feeling a bit lost. 

Since you want to keep the input variables light but focus heavily on extracting the chatbot's responses for validation, I've designed a profile that does exactly that. I added just one simple variable (`{{device}}`) to give the conversations a tiny bit of realistic variety, and I set up a robust `chatbot.output` section so you can easily write Sensei-check rules later to verify the support provided.

Here is a ready-to-use profile for your Erasmus student:

```yaml
test_name: "erasmus_student_setup_support"

llm:
  temperature: 0.7
  model: gpt-4o
  model_prov: openai
  format:
    type: text

user:
  language: English
  role: >
    You are an Erasmus exchange student who just arrived at the university. 
    You are feeling a bit overwhelmed and need help setting up your digital accounts 
    like Moodle, Sigma, and the campus Wi-Fi.
  context:
    - "You don't know your university credentials yet or how to activate them."
    - "You need clear, step-by-step instructions because you are unfamiliar with the local systems."
  goals:
    - "Ask how to activate your university email and get your initial password."
    - "Ask how to log in to Moodle to see your classes."
    - "Ask how to access Sigma to check your academic record."
    - "Ask how to connect your {{device}} to the campus Wi-Fi (eduroam)."
    - device:
        function: random()
        type: string
        data: [iPhone, Android phone, Mac laptop, Windows laptop]

chatbot:
  is_starter: false
  fallback: "I'm sorry, could you please rephrase your question? I am the IT support bot."
  output:
    - credential_activation_steps:
        type: string
        description: "Step-by-step instructions on how to activate the university account and get the password."
    - moodle_link:
        type: string
        description: "The URL or instructions provided to access Moodle."
    - sigma_instructions:
        type: string
        description: "Instructions or link provided to access the Sigma platform."
    - wifi_setup_guide:
        type: string
        description: "Instructions for connecting the specific device to the campus Wi-Fi."
    - support_contact:
        type: string
        description: "Email, phone number, or physical office location for the IT helpdesk in case of further issues."

conversation:
  number: 4
  goal_style:
    all_answered:
      export: true
      limit: 20
  interaction_style:
    - random:
      - make spelling mistakes
      - change language:
        - Spanish
      - single questions
```

### 🧐 Why this setup works perfectly for your needs:

1. **Rich `chatbot.output`:** This is the most important part for your goal! By defining fields like `moodle_link`, `sigma_instructions`, and `support_contact`, Sensei will extract exactly what the bot answered at the end of the conversation. Later, you can write Sensei Rules like `when: conv[0].moodle_link is None then: fail` to ensure the bot is actually doing its job!
2. **`all_answered` Goal Style:** I set the conversation to run until all the user's goals are answered. I also added `export: true`, which will generate a handy `.csv` dataframe showing exactly which outputs were successfully satisfied during the test.
3. **Realistic Interaction Styles:** Since they are an Erasmus student, I added a `random` interaction style where they might occasionally switch to Spanish, make spelling mistakes, or ask questions one by one. This mimics a real international student trying to navigate a new environment! 🌍
4. **Minimal Variables:** We only used one variable (`{{device}}`) with a `random()` function just to make sure the bot can handle Wi-Fi instructions for different operating systems without overcomplicating your test matrix.

You can save this as `profiles/erasmus_setup.yml` in your project folder.
