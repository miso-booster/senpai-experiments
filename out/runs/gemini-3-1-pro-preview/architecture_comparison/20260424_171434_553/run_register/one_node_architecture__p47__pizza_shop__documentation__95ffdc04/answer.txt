In the context of Sensei, a **metamorphic rule** is a clever way to test your chatbot by comparing **two or more conversations** against each other, rather than just looking at a single conversation in isolation. 

Usually, testing a chatbot is hard because you don't always know the *exact* right answer the bot should give (this is known as the "oracle problem"). Metamorphic rules solve this by checking the **relationship** between inputs and outputs instead of absolute values.

Here is a simple everyday analogy: 🍕
Imagine you don't know the exact price of a pizza at a new restaurant. However, you *do* know that if you order a medium pizza with 2 toppings, and then order that exact same medium pizza but with **4 toppings**, the second order should cost **more** than the first. 

That is exactly what a metamorphic rule does! In Sensei-check, you write these rules in YAML. They typically have three main parts:
1. **`when` (The Filter):** Selects comparable conversations (e.g., "Find two conversations where the pizza size and drink are the exact same").
2. **`if` (The Condition):** Looks at the change in input (e.g., "If conversation A has more toppings than conversation B...").
3. **`then` (The Expected Relation):** Asserts how the output should change (e.g., "...then the total price in conversation A must be higher than in conversation B").

By comparing sets of conversations, metamorphic rules help you ensure your chatbot's logic remains consistent and robust, even when you don't know the exact answers it will generate!
