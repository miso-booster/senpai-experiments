In the world of software testing and **Sensei-check**, an **oracle** is basically your "source of truth." It is the condition or rule we use to determine whether the chatbot's behavior is correct or incorrect. 

When you write a rule in Sensei, the oracle is the actual Python expression that evaluates to either `True` (the test passed) or `False` (the test failed). 

In Sensei-check, oracles usually fall into three categories:

1. **Traditional Oracles (Single Conversation):** These check an absolute condition in just one conversation. For example, "Did the chatbot ask for my address?" or "Is the price of a small pizza at least $10?"
2. **Metamorphic Oracles (Multiple Conversations):** Instead of checking an absolute answer, these check the *relationship* between two or more conversations. For example, "If I order the exact same pizza but add extra cheese, did the price go up?" (Note: In Sensei, we often use `if` and `then` keywords for these to make them easier to read, but they are still acting as the oracle!).
3. **Global Oracles (All Conversations):** These check a condition across your entire batch of tests, like making sure every single `order_id` generated is unique.

Here is a quick example of how an `oracle` looks inside a Sensei-check rule. This is a traditional, single-conversation oracle that checks if a small pizza costs at least $10:

```yaml
name: small_base_price_floor
description: Small pizzas should cost at least $10
conversations: 1
when: size == 'small'
oracle: extract_float(price) >= 10 and currency(price) == 'USD'
```

In short, the oracle is the brain of your rule that says, *"Yes, the chatbot did a good job!"* or *"Nope, something went wrong here!"* 👀
