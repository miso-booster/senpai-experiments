Since you want to check specific, absolute values for a single conversation, we'll create a **single-conversation oracle** rule. 

To make this rule robust, we can use a couple of Sensei-check's built-in helpers:
* `extract_float()`: This is perfect for your `tune_up`, `seat_price`, and `tire` fields. It will pull the number out even if the bot adds currency symbols like "$" or "€".
* `semantic_content()`: Since `opening_and_closing` is a string, the bot might reply with natural language (like *"We are open from 10:00 to 20:00"*). This helper uses an LLM under the hood to check if the meaning matches "10 to 20", saving you from writing complex regular expressions!

Here is the rule you can use:

```yaml
name: check_qa_prices_and_hours
description: Verifies that the opening hours are 10 to 20, tune-up is 50, seat is 20, and tire is 20.
conversations: 1
active: true
oracle: >
  semantic_content(opening_and_closing, '10 to 20') and
  extract_float(tune_up) == 50 and
  extract_float(seat_price) == 20 and
  extract_float(tire) == 20
yields: f"Failed validation! Got hours: {opening_and_closing}, tune-up: {tune_up}, seat: {seat_price}, tire: {tire}"
```

You can save this in your `rules/` folder. Whenever you run Sensei-check against the conversations generated by your `qa.yml` profile, it will automatically verify that the chatbot gave the correct prices and hours.
