Hello there! 🚲 I'd be absolutely delighted to help you write a Sensei-check rule for your bike shop profile! 

Since you want to verify specific, fixed values for a single chat (like the exact price of a tire or the specific opening hours), we will create a **single-conversation oracle**. This type of rule looks at one conversation at a time and checks if the extracted outputs match your expected values.

To do this, we'll use the exact field names you defined in your profile's `chatbot.output` (`opening_and_closing`, `tire`, `tune_up`, and `seat_price`). We can also use some handy built-in helpers:
* `extract_float()` to easily grab the numbers out of the price strings.
* `semantic_content()` to use a quick LLM check to ensure the opening hours mean "10 to 20", even if the bot phrases it like "We open at 10 AM and close at 8 PM (20:00)".

Here is the ready-to-use YAML rule for your project:

```yaml
name: check_bike_shop_info
description: Verifies that the bike shop assistant provides the correct prices and opening hours.
conversations: 1
oracle: extract_float(tune_up) == 50 and extract_float(seat_price) == 20 and extract_float(tire) == 20 and semantic_content(opening_and_closing, '10 to 20')
```

### 💡 A quick tip from Senpai:
If you want to know exactly *which* item failed during testing, you can also split this into two smaller rules—one for the prices and one for the schedule! 

For example, here is how the prices-only rule would look:

```yaml
name: check_bike_shop_prices
description: Verifies the prices of the tune-up, seat, and tire.
conversations: 1
oracle: extract_float(tune_up) == 50 and extract_float(seat_price) == 20 and extract_float(tire) == 20
```

And the schedule rule:

```yaml
name: check_bike_shop_hours
description: Verifies the opening and closing times.
conversations: 1
oracle: semantic_content(opening_and_closing, 'open from 10 to 20')
```
