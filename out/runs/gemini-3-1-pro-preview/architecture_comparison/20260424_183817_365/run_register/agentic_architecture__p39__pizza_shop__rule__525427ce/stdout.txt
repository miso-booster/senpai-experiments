Here is the YAML for your rule. It compares two conversations where everything else is the same, but one includes a drink and the other doesn't:

```yaml
name: drink_costs_2_dollars_extra
description: Adding a drink to an order increases the total price by exactly 2 dollars.
conversations: 2
when: conv[0].size == conv[1].size and conv[0].pizza_type == conv[1].pizza_type
if: conv[0].drink is not None and conv[1].drink is None
then: extract_float(conv[0].price) == extract_float(conv[1].price) + 2
```

### How this rule works:
* **`conversations: 2`**: This tells SENSEI-check that it's a metamorphic rule comparing a pair of conversations.
* **`when`**: This is our filter. It ensures we are comparing "apples to apples" (e.g., the pizza `size` and `pizza_type` must be identical in both conversations). *Note: You should adjust these fields to match whatever other items your actual `chatbot.output` tracks!*
* **`if`**: This is the trigger condition. It looks for a pair where the first conversation (`conv[0]`) has a `drink` and the second conversation (`conv[1]`) does not.
* **`then`**: This is the relation we are asserting. We use the built-in `extract_float()` helper to safely parse the numbers from the `price` field and check that the first conversation costs exactly $2 more than the second.

### A quick note on your Profile (`chatbot.output`):
For this rule to work, your SENSEI Chat profile must expose at least these fields in its `chatbot.output`:
1. `drink` (e.g., the name of the drink, or `None` if no drink was ordered)
2. `price` (the total cost of the order)
3. Any fields used in the `when` clause (like `size` or `pizza_type`) to ensure the rest of the order is identical.

If your `drink` output is actually a **list** of drinks rather than a single string, you can easily change the `if` condition to compare the length of the lists instead:
