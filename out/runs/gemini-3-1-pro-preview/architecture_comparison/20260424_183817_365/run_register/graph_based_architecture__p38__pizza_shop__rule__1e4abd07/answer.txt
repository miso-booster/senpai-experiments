Since we just want to verify that the chatbot successfully provided the `order_id` in a specific chat, we'll use a **single-conversation rule** (sometimes called a traditional oracle). 

Here is exactly how you would write it in YAML:

```yaml
name: order_id_provided
description: Ensures the chatbot successfully provides an order ID to the user.
conversations: 1
oracle: order_id is not None
```

### 🕵️‍♂️ Let's break down what's happening here:
* **`conversations: 1`**: This tells Sensei-check that we are looking at each conversation individually, rather than comparing multiple conversations together.
* **`oracle: order_id is not None`**: This is our simple Python-style check! It looks at the extracted outputs and makes sure that the `order_id` actually has a value and isn't empty/missing. 

*(Note: You could also write `oracle: "'order_id' in data_output"` if you prefer checking the raw output dictionary, but checking the variable directly is usually cleaner!)*

### 💡 Senpai's Pro-Tip:
For this rule to work its magic, remember that your **SENSEI Chat profile** needs to have `order_id` listed in its `chatbot.output` section. Sensei-check reads directly from those profile outputs to evaluate your rules!
