To make the simulated user change their mind halfway through, we can use a combination of two things:
1. Setting the **`interaction_style`** to `[change your mind]`. This tells the simulator to naturally hesitate or alter its decisions during the chat.
2. Structuring the **`goals`** and **`context`** so the user explicitly starts with a custom pizza request and then pivots to a predefined one.

Here is a ready-to-use profile for your scenario:

```yaml
test_name: "change_mind_custom_to_predefined"

llm:
  temperature: 0.7
  model: gpt-4o-mini
  format:
    type: text

user:
  language: English
  role: "A customer ordering a pizza who is a bit indecisive."
  context:
    - "You initially want to build your own custom pizza."
    - "Halfway through the conversation, you decide it's too much hassle or you spot something better, and you change your mind to order a predefined pizza instead."
  goals:
    - "Start by ordering a custom pizza with {{toppings}}."
    - "Change your mind and ask for a {{predefined_pizza}} pizza instead."
    - toppings:
        function: random()
        type: string
        data: [extra cheese and mushrooms, pepperoni and onions, ham and olives]
    - predefined_pizza:
        function: random()
        type: string
        data: [Margherita, Pepperoni, Hawaiian, Vegetarian]

chatbot:
  is_starter: false
  fallback: "I'm sorry, I didn't catch that. Could you repeat your pizza order?"
  output:
    - initial_request:
        type: string
        description: "The custom pizza toppings the user initially asked for before changing their mind."
    - final_pizza_ordered:
        type: string
        description: "The final predefined pizza the user settled on after changing their mind."

conversation:
  number: 3
  goal_style:
    all_answered:
      limit: 15
  interaction_style:
    - change your mind
```

### Why this works:
* **`interaction_style: [change your mind]`**: This built-in feature makes the LLM act indecisively, perfectly matching your requirement.
* **`chatbot.output`**: I added two fields (`initial_request` and `final_pizza_ordered`) so that when the conversation ends, you can easily verify that the chatbot successfully tracked the change in the user's order! 👀

Let me know if you'd like to adjust the toppings, the pizza types, or anything else! 😊
