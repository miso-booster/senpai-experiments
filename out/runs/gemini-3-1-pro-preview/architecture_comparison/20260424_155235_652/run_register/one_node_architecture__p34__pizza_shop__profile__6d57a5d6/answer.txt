To make the simulated user change their mind halfway through, we can use a combination of explicit instructions in the `user.goals` and the built-in `change your mind` interaction style. This will make the user act naturally indecisive!

We'll also set up the `chatbot.output` to extract the `final_pizza_ordered` so you can verify if your chatbot successfully caught the change of plans. 👀

Here is the complete profile ready for your project:

```yaml
test_name: "change_mind_custom_to_predefined"

llm:
  temperature: 0.7
  model: gpt-4o-mini
  format:
    type: text

user:
  language: English
  role: "A hungry but indecisive customer ordering a pizza."
  context:
    - "You start by wanting to build your own custom pizza."
    - "During the conversation, you realize it's too much hassle or change your mind, and decide to just get a predefined classic pizza instead."
  goals:
    - "Start by asking to order a custom pizza with {{custom_toppings}}."
    - "Change your mind and tell the assistant you want a {{predefined_pizza}} pizza instead."
    - "Confirm the final order and ask for the total price."
    - custom_toppings:
        function: random()
        type: string
        data: 
          - "pepperoni and jalapenos"
          - "mushrooms, onions, and olives"
          - "extra cheese and bacon"
    - predefined_pizza:
        function: random()
        type: string
        data: 
          - "Margherita"
          - "Pepperoni"
          - "Vegetarian"
          - "Hawaiian"

chatbot:
  is_starter: false
  fallback: "I'm sorry, I didn't catch that. Would you like a custom or predefined pizza?"
  output:
    - final_pizza_ordered:
        type: string
        description: "The actual pizza the user settled on at the end of the conversation."
    - total_price:
        type: currency
        description: "The final price of the order."

conversation:
  number: 3
  goal_style:
    all_answered:
      export: false
      limit: 15
  interaction_style:
    - change your mind
```

### 💡 A quick breakdown of what's happening here:
* **`interaction_style: [change your mind]`**: This tells the Sensei user simulator to behave indecisively, which perfectly fits your scenario!
* **Variables (`{{custom_toppings}}` & `{{predefined_pizza}}`)**: We use the `random()` function so that across the 3 conversations (`conversation.number: 3`), the user will try different combinations of toppings and predefined pizzas.
* **`chatbot.output`**: By extracting `final_pizza_ordered`, you can later write a **Sensei Rule** to check if the chatbot correctly registered the `predefined_pizza` instead of the custom one.
