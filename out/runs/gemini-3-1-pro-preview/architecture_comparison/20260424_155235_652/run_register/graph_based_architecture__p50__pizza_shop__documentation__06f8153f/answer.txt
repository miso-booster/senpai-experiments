In the world of **SENSEI RULES** (specifically Sensei-check), an **oracle** is essentially a "judge" or a test condition that decides whether your chatbot behaved correctly during a conversation. 

Think of it as a statement of truth that *must* be met. If the oracle's condition evaluates to `True`, the test passes! If it evaluates to `False`, the test fails, meaning the chatbot didn't do what it was supposed to do.

You write an oracle in your rule's YAML file using Python-like expressions and our built-in helper functions. 

Here are the two main ways oracles are used:

**1. Single-Conversation Oracles (Traditional)**
These look at just *one* conversation at a time to check for baseline sanity or specific behaviors. 
*Example:* Making sure the chatbot actually collected all the required data from the user.
```yaml
name: chatbot_fills_all_slots
description: All conversation slots are filled
conversations: 1
oracle: data_collected() == True
```

**2. Global Oracles**
These look at *all* the conversations in your dataset at once to check for global properties.
*Example:* Making sure every single order ID generated by the bot is completely unique across all tests.
```yaml
name: unique_order_ids
description: Every conversation has a unique order_id
conversations: all
oracle: is_unique('order_id')
```

*(Note: When we compare two or more conversations against each other—which we call **metamorphic testing**—we often use `if` and `then` statements instead of a single `oracle` keyword, because it reads a bit more clearly like a law. But the concept is exactly the same!)*
