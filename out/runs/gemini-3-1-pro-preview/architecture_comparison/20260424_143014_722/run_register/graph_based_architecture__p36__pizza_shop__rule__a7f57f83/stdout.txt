Hello there! I'd be happy to help you fix this SENSEI RULE. 😊 It looks like a classic case of a missing puzzle piece!

In Sensei-check, whenever you use a `then` statement to check an expected outcome, it **must** be paired with an `if` statement that sets up the precondition. 

Right now, your rule's description says *"Adding more toppings... costs more"*, but the rule itself is missing the part that actually checks the toppings! Because the `if` is missing, Sensei-check doesn't know what triggers the `then` condition.

Here is the fixed version of your rule:

```yaml
name: more_toppings_cost_more
description: Adding more toppings to a custom pizza costs more
active: True
conversations: 2
when: conv[0].size == conv[1].size and conv[0].drink == conv[1].drink
if: len(conv[0].toppings) > len(conv[1].toppings)
then: extract_float(conv[0].price) > extract_float(conv[1].price)
```

### 🧠 A quick way to think about it:
* **`when`** is like the bouncer at the door: It filters the conversations, only letting in pairs that have the exact same pizza size and drink.
* **`if`** is the specific scenario we are testing: Out of those matching pairs, does one have *more* toppings than the other?
* **`then`** is the expected outcome: If it has more toppings, the price must be higher! 🍕💸
