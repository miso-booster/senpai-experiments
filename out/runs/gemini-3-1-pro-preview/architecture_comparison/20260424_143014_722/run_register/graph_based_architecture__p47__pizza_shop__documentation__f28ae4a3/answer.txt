In the context of Sensei-check, a **metamorphic rule** is a clever way to test your chatbot by comparing *multiple* conversations against each other, rather than just looking at one conversation in isolation. 🕵️‍♂️✨

Normally, testing a chatbot is hard because you don't always know the exact perfect answer it should give (this is known as the "oracle problem"). Metamorphic testing solves this by checking the **logical relationship** between different conversations instead of looking for an absolute answer.

### A Simple Everyday Analogy 🍕
Imagine you are ordering a pizza from a chatbot:
1. In **Conversation A**, you order a medium pizza with **2 toppings**.
2. In **Conversation B**, you order a medium pizza with **4 toppings**.

Even if you don't know the exact price of the pizzas, logic tells you that **Conversation B should cost more than Conversation A**. 

That is exactly what a metamorphic rule does! It asserts how outputs should change (or stay the same) when inputs change.

### How it works in Sensei
You write these rules in YAML format. A typical metamorphic rule has three main parts:
* **`conversations`**: How many conversations you want to compare (usually `2` or `all`).
* **`when` (The Filter)**: This selects the conversations that are fair to compare (e.g., "only compare pizzas of the same size").
* **`if` / `then` (The Relation)**: The actual rule you are testing (e.g., "If pizza A has more toppings than pizza B, then pizza A's price must be higher").

Here is a quick peek at what that looks like in code:
```yaml
name: toppings_monotonicity
description: Adding more toppings to a custom pizza costs more
conversations: 2
when: conv[0].size == conv[1].size     # Keep the size the same!
if:   len(conv[0].toppings) > len(conv[1].toppings)
then: extract_float(conv[0].price) > extract_float(conv[1].price)
```

It’s a super powerful way to make sure your chatbot behaves consistently and logically!
